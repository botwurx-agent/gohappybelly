## The Part Nobody Mentions

Everyone talks about the gut. Fewer people talk about where the gut sends
things, which is a problem, because clearance is half the job.

Your liver filters the blood coming out of your gut. Everything absorbed from
your digestive tract goes there first, through a vein called the portal vein,
before it reaches the rest of you. The liver sorts it, processes what needs
processing, packages waste, and sends it back to the gut to leave.

So the two are a loop, not a sequence. If either end is slow, both back up.

## What The Liver Handles

Used hormones. Things you ate, drank, breathed or put on your skin. Byproducts
of your own metabolism. It converts them into forms that can be carried out, and
sends most of that out through bile.

## Bile

Bile is made by the liver, stored in the gallbladder, and released when fat
arrives. It does two jobs at once: it lets you digest fat, and it carries
processed waste out of the body.

Which means sluggish bile is both a digestion problem and a clearance problem.
That shows up as feeling heavy or nauseated after rich food, and as the sense
that something is not moving.

> **Alina's take.** The liver is the sink and the gut is the drain. People spend
> a lot of money scrubbing the sink. If the drain is blocked, you are rinsing
> the same water around and around, and the harder you scrub the worse it looks.
>
> This is why I am skeptical of cleanses that do not start with whether you are
> going to the bathroom every day. Opening the drain first is unglamorous and it
> is the step that makes everything after it work.

## Signs Worth Noticing

- Bloating or constipation
- Skin breakouts or rashes
- Fatigue or headaches
- Hormone-related symptoms through the month

The hormone one connects back to module two. Used estrogen leaves through this
route. If the route is slow, some of it is unpacked and reabsorbed instead of
leaving.

## What Supports It

- **Daily bowel movements.** This is first for a reason. Everything else
  depends on it
- Water and fiber
- Cruciferous vegetables: broccoli, cabbage, Brussels sprouts
- Enough healthy fat to prompt bile release
- Protein, because the liver's processing work requires amino acids
- Rest and stress management

**On that protein point.** Low-protein diets slow this work down, which is one
reason very restrictive cleanses can leave people feeling worse while believing
they are doing something good. Whole food beats a harsh protocol here, reliably.

## When To Look Further

Fatigue or skin issues that persist, chronic constipation, new sensitivity to
smells, alcohol or supplements. Functional testing can see things that guessing
cannot.
