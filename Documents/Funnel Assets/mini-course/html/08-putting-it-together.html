Four Things, In Order

Everything in this course sorts into four:

  1. Nourish. Real food, fiber, enough protein
  2. Support. Give digestion the conditions it needs, which is mostly calm and chewing
  3. Balance. Sleep and stress, because they set the mode everything else runs in
  4. Clear. Keep things moving out

The order is not decorative. If you are not clearing, nourishing harder does not help. If you are eating in fight-or-flight, the quality of the food matters less than it should.

Why Small Wins

You will be tempted to do all four at once. Most people are, and most people last about eleven days.

One change, held until it stops requiring effort, then the next. That is slower on paper and faster in practice, and it has the advantage that when something improves you know what caused it.

A Day That Works

  • Morning. Water before coffee. Three slow breaths
  • Meals. Sitting down. Chew more than feels necessary
  • Midday. Move. Drink
  • Evening. Lighter dinner, earlier. Wind down properly

That is not a protocol. It is a shape, and you fit your life into it rather than the reverse.

Tracking Without Obsessing

Watch trends, not days. A bad Tuesday means nothing. Four bad Tuesdays in a row after the same thing means something.

Note three things: energy, mood, digestion. A line a day is enough. The point is to be able to look back in a month and see a direction, because you will not remember accurately otherwise.

Alina's take. Think of it as an orchestra. Every habit is one instrument, and none of them sounds like much alone. What you are listening for is whether the whole thing is starting to play together, and that is not something you can hear on any given day.

When Habits Are Not Enough

I want to be honest about the limits of what you just learned.

The foundations in this course help almost everyone, and for some people they are genuinely sufficient. For others they are necessary and not sufficient. You will know which you are by this signal: you are doing the work consistently, for a reasonable stretch, and the symptoms keep coming back.

That is not a discipline problem and it does not mean you did it wrong. It means something specific is going on that general support does not reach, and no amount of additional general support will.

At that point the question stops being "what should I do" and becomes "what is actually happening." Those are different questions and they need different tools: knowing your pattern, and where appropriate, functional testing that shows what is there rather than inferring it.
