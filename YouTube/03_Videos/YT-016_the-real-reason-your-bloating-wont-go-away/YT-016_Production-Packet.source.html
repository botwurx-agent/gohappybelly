2:55 – 4:25Point 1 · It's not a food, it's a neighborhood

Your gut is supposed to work like a house with very clear rooms. Your large intestine is where most of your bacteria are meant to live, in large numbers, doing useful work. Your small intestine, right before it, is supposed to stay relatively quiet, with far fewer bacteria, because that is where you actually absorb your nutrients.

Now, when bloating will not quit no matter what you cut, one pattern I see over and over is this. Some of those bacteria have drifted into the wrong room. Not new bacteria, not bad bacteria, the same bacteria that are supposed to be there, just living somewhere they do not belong. When that happens, they start fermenting your food earlier than they are supposed to, right where you are trying to absorb it. That fermentation makes gas. The gas makes you bloat, often within thirty minutes or so of eating, sometimes so much you look pregnant by the end of the day.

7:10 – 8:45Point 4 · The signs this pattern is yours

So how do you know if this is actually what is going on with you? Here are the signs I look for.

One. The bloating shows up fast, often within thirty to sixty minutes of eating, and it gets visibly worse as the day goes on. Flatter in the morning, distended by evening.

Two. Certain healthy foods make it worse, not better. Beans, onions, garlic, certain fruits, the exact foods that get labeled "gut healthy" everywhere online. That is often a clue you are dealing with fermentation happening in the wrong place, not a food that is bad for you in general.

Three. It came on, or got dramatically worse, after a round of food poisoning, a course of antibiotics, or a stretch of high stress. Any of the things that can disrupt the normal cleanup rhythm of your gut.

Four. You have tried an elimination diet, felt better briefly, and relapsed with no clear new trigger, exactly the cycle we already walked through.

And five, the one almost nobody connects. For a lot of women with this pattern, probiotics make it worse, not better, at least at first. If adding "good bacteria" made your bloating spike, that is not you being broken. That is a strong clue this is a location issue, not a not-enough-good-bacteria issue.

8:45 – 12:35Point 5 · The 14-day reset that addresses it

So if the mop was never going to fix the leak, what does?

This is where I want to walk you through something I teach my clients, a 14-day reset built to give your system's natural cleanup rhythm a real chance to work again, instead of avoiding foods forever. I want to be clear about what this is. It is not a permanent restriction. It is a short, structured window, and it focuses on three things.

First, spacing. You give real space between meals, about four to five hours, instead of grazing all day. Your gut has a natural cleaning wave that runs between meals and sweeps leftover material through, and that wave barely gets a chance to run if you are never actually done eating.

Second, a temporary lower-fermentation way of eating for those fourteen days specifically. Not because those foods are bad, but because giving the wrong-room fermentation a break for two weeks is part of how the reset works. This is short-term and strategic, not a forever list.

Third, supporting your body's own digestive rhythm. Eating without rushing, chewing fully, and giving your body cues that it is safe to run its normal maintenance in between meals.

Let me make that real, because "space your meals" sounds simple until you try to live it. Here is what a day can look like. Breakfast around eight, a real breakfast with some protein in it, not a coffee and a granola bar. Then nothing but water or a plain herbal tea until lunch, around one. Lunch, sitting down, phone away. Then a gap again until dinner, around six or six thirty. And then the kitchen closes, ideally a few hours before bed, so the cleanup wave gets a long, quiet stretch overnight to do its best work.

That is it. Three real meals, real gaps. No grazing, no "just a bite" while you cook, no little snack at three because it is three. If you are someone who gets shaky or lightheaded going four hours without food, that is worth paying attention to, and it is a sign to build up to longer gaps slowly instead of white-knuckling it. The point is not to suffer. The point is to give your gut a shift it has not had in a long time.

On screen: HEALTHY SNACKS. NO CLEANUP SHIFT.

Mistake number two, turning the reset into a life sentence. The lower-fermentation part is meant to be short. When it turns into a permanent list, you end up eating fewer and fewer foods, which is hard on your gut and hard on your head.
