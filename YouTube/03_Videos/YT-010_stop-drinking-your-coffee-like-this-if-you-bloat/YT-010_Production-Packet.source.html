Section 1

The Script

Length: 17 to 19 min  ·  Format: solo to camera  ·  One idea: bloating after coffee is rarely about the coffee, it is about drinking it fasted, too early, in too much volume, and stacked on poor sleep, which throws a stressed system's timing off.
Delivery: conversational and warm. This is NOT an anti-coffee video, say so early and mean it. The small side-notes and colored labels are for editing, do not read them aloud.
0:00 – 0:40Hook

I am not going to tell you to quit coffee. I want to say that in the first ten seconds because every other video on this topic is secretly building up to "and that is why you need to give it up," and I know that is not what you clicked on, and it is not what I am about to tell you.

On screen: Keep your coffee. Fix this.

What I am going to show you is why coffee makes some women bloated, wired, and strangely more anxious by ten a.m., while other women drink the exact same coffee and feel completely fine. It is almost never the coffee. It is four specific habits around how and when you are drinking it, and by the end I am going to give you the one small change that undoes most of it without you giving up a single cup.

Editor  Open the "one small change" loop now, pay it off at Point 5. B-roll: a coffee pot pouring, a hand reaching for a mug before anything else has happened that morning, a clock.
0:40 – 1:20Credential moment

I am Alina Nazari, I am a Functional Diagnostic Nutrition Practitioner, and most of the women I work with are exactly the kind of busy, wired, running-on-fumes woman who would rather skip breakfast than skip coffee. So I am not coming at this from some anti-caffeine place. I drink coffee. This is not that video.

What I am coming at this from is years of watching one specific pattern show up over and over in women who feel bloated, tired, and strangely on edge, all at the same time. And coffee, drunk a certain way, is one of the clearest triggers for it.

Visual  Warm, direct to camera. Lower third with name and FDN-P appears here.
1:20 – 3:15Why it hits some women and not others

Here is what I hear constantly. "I wake up already tired. I need coffee just to feel like a person. I drink it, and for a minute I feel sharp, and then an hour later my stomach is upset, I am bloated, and I am somehow more anxious than before I had it." And the advice out there is always the same, cut back on caffeine, switch to decaf, just deal with being tired. Which, if you are already running on empty, is not advice, it is a punishment.

And here is what nobody tells this woman. Her body is not rejecting coffee. Her body is already running like a car with the engine timing off, every system firing a little out of sequence, tired but wired, hungry but nauseous, wanting to sleep but unable to. And then she pours caffeine into that system, first thing, on an empty stomach, before anything else has had a chance to settle, and it is like flooring the gas pedal on an engine that is already misfiring. Of course something backfires.

On screen: It's not the coffee

It is not the coffee. It is the timing. And once you see the four habits that throw the timing off, you can keep the coffee and fix the actual problem.

3:15 – 6:00Point 1 · The engine timing problem

Let me explain what I mean by timing, because this is the whole video in one idea.

Picture a car engine. When the timing is right, the spark fires at exactly the right moment in the cycle, and everything runs smooth. When the timing is off, even a little, the same fuel, the same spark plug, the same engine produces knocking, stalling, rough idle. Nothing about the fuel changed. The sequence did.

Your nervous system runs your digestion the same way. Stomach acid, digestive enzymes, gut motility, they are all supposed to fire in a sequence, and that sequence is controlled by whether your nervous system thinks it is safe to rest and digest, or whether it thinks it needs to be on alert. When you are chronically stressed, under-slept, and running on adrenaline just to get through a normal Tuesday, that system starts firing out of sequence before you have had a single sip of coffee.

On screen: The engine timing is off

This is what I call the Wired-and-Tired pattern, and here is the line I want you to remember, because it explains everything that is about to make sense.

"The food is fine. The system processing it isn't."

Key line  Deliver this slowly, it reframes coffee, food, and everything that follows. B-roll: non-clinical animation of an engine with the timing slipping, then the same idea mapped onto a body silhouette. Never imply a diagnosed disease.
6:00 – 9:00Point 2 · Habit 1, drinking it fasted, first thing

Habit one. You wake up and the very first thing that goes into your body is coffee, on a completely empty stomach, before you have eaten anything at all.

Here is why that lands so hard on a Wired-and-Tired system specifically. Caffeine tells your adrenal glands to release cortisol and adrenaline. That is literally the mechanism, that is how it wakes you up. If your nervous system is already leaning stressed, you are adding a stress hormone spike on top of a stress hormone spike, on a stomach that has nothing in it to buffer any of it. Stomach acid ramps up with nowhere to go. Your gut gets the signal to speed up before it has anything to actually move. That combination is exactly what shows up as that fast, uncomfortable, almost immediate bloating and stomach upset some women get within twenty minutes of their first sip.

On screen: Fasted coffee = double stress signal

Notice this is not "coffee is acidic, therefore bad." Plenty of women drink coffee on an empty stomach their whole lives with zero issue, because their system was not already running wired and tired to begin with. Same fuel, different engine. That is the whole point.

Editor  Split screen, a mug next to an empty plate versus a mug next to a small bowl of food. Keep tone matter-of-fact, not alarming.
9:00 – 11:45Point 3 · Habit 2, too early and too much

Habit two, and this one surprises people. It is not just that you drink coffee early, it is how early, and how much, relative to what your body is already doing on its own.

Your body actually produces its own natural cortisol spike in the first thirty to sixty minutes after you wake up, it is called the cortisol awakening response, and it is supposed to be what gets you moving. If you are chugging a big coffee the second your eyes open, you are stacking a caffeine-driven cortisol spike directly on top of your body's own natural one, at the exact moment it is already highest. For a Wired-and-Tired system, that is not a gentle nudge awake, that is two alarms going off at once.

And then there is volume. One cup on a calm system is very different from three cups by ten a.m. on a system that is already running hot. Each cup is another spark firing into an engine that has not had a chance to settle from the last one. The bloating, the jitteriness, the wired-but-exhausted feeling by lunchtime, a lot of that is simply too much fuel, too fast, on top of a system that was already misfiring.

On screen: Two alarms at once

This is not about never having a second cup. It is about noticing whether your system can actually handle the volume and timing you are giving it, or whether it is already maxed out before coffee even enters the picture.

Visual  A simple graphic, two rising lines (natural cortisol curve, caffeine curve) stacking into one tall spike. Keep it clean, not clinical.
11:45 – 14:15Point 4 · Habit 3, stacking it on poor sleep

Habit three, and this is the one I think matters most, because it is the one almost nobody connects to their gut. Drinking coffee, especially in the way we just described, on top of a night of bad or short sleep.

When you are under-slept, your nervous system is already starting the day in that alert, on-guard state, before coffee touches it. Poor sleep alone raises your baseline cortisol and throws off the natural rhythm your gut relies on to know when to rest and digest versus when to stay on alert. So you are not pouring caffeine into a calm, well-rested system that can absorb the jolt. You are pouring it into a system that spent the whole night already firing out of sequence, and now you are asking it to also process food on top of that.

This is why the exact same cup of coffee can feel totally fine on a week where you slept well, and absolutely wreck your stomach on a week where you did not. It was never really about the coffee being different. It was about how much timing damage was already there before the coffee showed up.

On screen: Bad sleep loads the engine first

And this is exactly why "just cut out coffee" so often does not fix it. If sleep is the real driver, you can quit coffee entirely and still bloat, because the underlying timing problem never got addressed.

Visual  A tired woman scrolling her phone late at night, then the same woman reaching for coffee the next morning. Warm, relatable, not shaming.
14:15 – 16:45Point 5 · How to keep your coffee and fix the timing

So here is the one small change I promised you, and then three more you can layer on if you want to go further.

The single biggest lever is this: do not let coffee be the first thing that enters your body. Have a few sips of water first, and if you can, a small bite of something with protein or fat in it before your first cup, even just a few bites. That alone gives your system a buffer so caffeine is not landing on a completely empty, already-alert stomach. For a lot of women this one change quiets down most of the fast, within-twenty-minutes bloating almost immediately.

Second, give yourself sixty to ninety minutes after waking before that first cup, so you are working with your natural cortisol rise instead of piling directly on top of it. Third, notice your actual volume. You do not need to quit coffee, you need to notice if you are somewhere around cup three by mid-morning on a system that is already wired, and consider whether cup one or two would actually get you the same lift with a lot less backfire. And fourth, on the nights you know sleep was rough, treat the next morning as a lower-caffeine morning on purpose, because that is the day your system has the least room to absorb the jolt.

On screen: Eat first, wait, watch the volume

None of this is about fear or restriction. It is about giving your engine's timing a chance to catch up before you ask it to also process a stimulant. Most women who make just the first change, eating or drinking water before that first cup, notice a real difference within a few days.

Payoff  This closes the loop from the hook. Practical and warm. Show a small bite of food and a glass of water next to the coffee mug on camera (also a clip).

Thumbnail Brief

For the designer. Modeled on the proven pattern (face + strong expression + a 2 to 4 word overlay + high contrast), rebuilt in the Go Happy Belly palette.

The one glance

Your coffee isn't the villain, how you're drinking it is. The viewer should feel "wait, I do that" and want the fix.

Text on the thumbnail

  • Primary (4 words max): IT'S NOT THE COFFEE
  • Secondary (smaller, terracotta): FIX THE TIMING

Visual concept

  • Subject: close crop of Alina, shoulders up, a knowing, slightly conspiratorial "let me tell you the real reason" expression, direct eye contact.
  • Background & color: solid navy (#1B2D4F) so face and text pop. Primary text in warm cream/white with a subtle dark outline. Secondary text in terracotta (#C67B5C). A thin sage (#7BA987) accent used sparingly.
  • Visual device (pick one): a coffee cup with a small clock/stopwatch face overlaid, or steam that forms a subtle clock hand, clean and bold, placed low and small. (Alt: a coffee cup icon with a small timing-gear graphic instead of a "no" ring, since the video is not anti-coffee.)

Layout

  • Face on the right ~45%, text stacked on the left third, coffee-cup device low and small.
  • Test at phone-thumbnail size first. If the words are not readable on a phone at a glance, cut a word.
  • Keep the same face framing, palette, and text style as YT-002 and YT-005 and future videos so the channel is recognizable in a grid.
  • Do not use a "no" or crossed-out symbol over the coffee cup. The video keeps the coffee, fixes the habit, and a crossed-out cup sends the opposite message before anyone clicks.
Compliance  No condition named, no "adrenal fatigue," no "cure" or "treat." On-brand palette, not Ben's yellow and red. Section 4

Shoot Day Checklist & Upload Copy

For Steve. Everything to film it and upload it.

1. Before the shoot

  • Alina reads the script out loud twice. Familiarity, not memorization.
  • Lock the 3 word-for-word lines: the hook opener ("I am not going to tell you to quit coffee"), the aha line ("The food is fine. The system processing it isn't."), and the close ("Your coffee was never really the enemy. The timing was.").
  • Wardrobe: solid warm tones (cream, sage, terracotta, navy). No busy patterns, no pure white or black, no big logos.
  • Location: quiet room, soft daylight or a key light, warm non-clinical background. A coffee mug as a prop is fine here, it is on-topic.
  • Water nearby, phone silent, notifications off. Have a mug and a small plate/bowl on set for the Point 5 demo.

2. Gear & setup

  • Camera at eye level, framed chest-up, eyes on the top third.
  • Light in front and slightly to one side, softened. Avoid overhead-only light.
  • Audio is the priority: lav or shotgun mic close to her, not the built-in mic. Record 10 seconds of room tone.
  • Teleprompter app just under or over the lens so her eyeline stays on camera. Slow scroll.
  • Do a 20-second test: focus, exposure, audio levels.

3. Delivery notes for Alina

  • Talk to one person, not an audience (someone who cannot function without her morning coffee and feels guilty reading "coffee is bad for you" headlines).
  • Say the "I am not telling you to quit coffee" line early with warmth and total conviction, not defensively.
  • Slow down on the hook and the aha line. Pauses read as confidence.
  • Energy slightly higher than feels natural (camera flattens it ~20%).
  • Fine to restart a sentence: pause, clap once (helps the editor), redo the line.
  • Warm and direct, never clinical. This is a "here is what is actually happening" video, not a scare video.

4. Shot list

A-roll: film each section as its own take: Hook, Credential, Problem, Points 1 to 5, Bridge/CTA, Outro.

B-roll (10 to 20 sec each, silent):

  • Coffee pot pouring, a hand reaching for a mug first thing in the morning (hook).
  • A rushed morning scene, an alarm going off, feet hitting the floor (problem).
  • A mug next to an empty plate, then a mug next to a small bowl of food (Point 2, also a clip).
  • A tired woman scrolling her phone late at night, then reaching for coffee the next morning (Point 4).
  • A small bite of food and a glass of water set next to the coffee mug (Point 5, also a clip).
