### Point 1: It's not a food, it's a neighborhood (2:55 to 4:25)

[SPOKEN]: Your gut is supposed to work like a house with very clear rooms. Your large intestine is where most of your bacteria are meant to live, in large numbers, doing useful work. Your small intestine, right before it, is supposed to stay relatively quiet, with far fewer bacteria, because that is where you actually absorb your nutrients.

[SPOKEN]: Now, when bloating will not quit no matter what you cut, one pattern I see over and over is this. Some of those bacteria have drifted into the wrong room. Not new bacteria, not bad bacteria, the same bacteria that are supposed to be there, just living somewhere they do not belong. When that happens, they start fermenting your food earlier than they are supposed to, right where you are trying to absorb it. That fermentation makes gas. The gas makes you bloat, often within thirty minutes or so of eating, sometimes so much you look pregnant by the end of the day.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: WRONG ROOM, NOT WRONG FOOD

[SPOKEN]: This is what I call the Bloated-and-Backed-Up pattern, bacteria that moved to the wrong neighborhood. And here is the line I want you to take with you, because it explains why cutting foods never fully worked.

[SPOKEN]: The bacteria aren't the problem. The location is.

### Point 5: The 14-day reset that addresses it (8:45 to 12:35)

[SPOKEN]: So if the mop was never going to fix the leak, what does?

I want to be clear about what this is. It is not a permanent restriction. It is a short, structured window, and it focuses on three things.

[SPOKEN]: First, spacing. You give real space between meals, about four to five hours, instead of grazing all day. Your gut has a natural cleaning wave that runs between meals and sweeps leftover material through, and that wave barely gets a chance to run if you are never actually done eating.

[SPOKEN]: Second, a temporary lower-fermentation way of eating for those fourteen days specifically. Not because those foods are bad, but because giving the wrong-room fermentation a break for two weeks is part of how the reset works. This is short-term and strategic, not a forever list.

[SPOKEN]: Third, supporting your body's own digestive rhythm. Eating without rushing, chewing fully, and giving your body cues that it is safe to run its normal maintenance in between meals.

[SPOKEN]: Let me make that real, because "space your meals" sounds simple until you try to live it. Here is what a day can look like. Lunch, sitting down, phone away. Then a gap again until dinner, around six or six thirty.
