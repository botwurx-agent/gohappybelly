### Point 1: Where the bacteria actually live (3:00 to 6:00)

[SPOKEN]: Here is the piece that reframes everything. Your gut is supposed to have bacteria living in it, that part is completely normal and healthy. The problem is not that bacteria exist. The problem is where they end up.

[SPOKEN]: Your large intestine is supposed to be the neighborhood where most of that bacterial population lives and does its fermenting. Your small intestine, right above it, is supposed to stay comparatively quiet, mostly focused on absorbing your food. When bacteria that belong in the large intestine end up relocating into the small intestine, you get gas and bloating produced in a space that was never built to handle that much fermentation, right where it presses against everything around it. That is why it can feel like the bloating shows up almost the moment you eat, and why it can feel so much bigger than "a normal amount of gas" should feel.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: WRONG NEIGHBORHOOD, NOT WRONG FOOD

[SPOKEN]: This is what I call the Bloated-and-Backed-Up pattern, and I want you to remember one line, because it is the whole video in one sentence.

[SPOKEN]: The bacteria aren't the problem. The location is.

[RETENTION LOOP]: This is the locked aha line, deliver it slowly. It closes the loop from the hook and reframes the entire video.

### Point 2: Why elimination diets keep failing you (6:00 to 8:30)

[SPOKEN]: So now you can see why cutting out food after food never fully solves this. When you remove a trigger food, you are removing some of the fuel those relocated bacteria ferment, so you often do feel a little better for a while. But you have not moved the bacteria back to where they belong. You have just changed what you are feeding them in the wrong location.

[SPOKEN]: Think of it like a flooded basement with a leak that never got repaired. You can mop up the water, and the floor looks dry for a few days, and you feel like you handled it. But if the leak itself is still there, the water is going to come back, usually when you least expect it, and you are right back to mopping. That is the recurrence problem so many women run into. The diet "worked," and then a few weeks or months later, it stopped working, and it was never really about that food to begin with.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: MOPPING THE FLOOR, NOT FIXING THE LEAK

[SPOKEN]: This is why you can eliminate an entire aisle of the grocery store and still end up bloated. You were addressing the water on the floor. Nobody showed you the leak.

[VISUAL NOTE]: Split screen, a woman mopping a floor while water keeps trickling in behind her. Keep it visual and a little wry, not alarming.

### Point 3: Your gut has a built-in cleanup crew (8:30 to 11:30)

[SPOKEN]: Here is the good news, and it is the part that actually gets you somewhere. Your digestive system has its own built-in housekeeping wave. Between meals, once your stomach and small intestine are done processing what you just ate, they run a strong sweeping motion that pushes leftover food particles and bacteria down and out, back toward where they belong. Think of it as a cleanup crew that comes through the hallway once everyone has gone home for the night.

[SPOKEN]: The catch is that this cleanup crew only comes out when the hallway is actually empty. The moment you eat or drink anything with calories, even a "healthy" snack, that sweeping motion stops and digestion starts back up again. So if you are grazing all day, a few nuts at ten, a protein bar at noon, crackers at three, dinner at six, a little something at eight, the cleanup crew never gets a real shift to work. It keeps getting sent home the second it clocks in.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: THE CLEANUP CREW NEEDS AN EMPTY HALLWAY

[SPOKEN]: And this is the part that connects everything back to the leak from a minute ago. That housekeeping wave is one of the main ways your body naturally keeps bacteria from relocating and building up where they should not be. When it never runs, the "leak" stays unrepaired, no matter how carefully you eat.

[RETENTION LOOP]: Sets up the payoff for Point 4, "so what do I actually do about it."
[VISUAL NOTE]: Simple animation, a hallway light sweeping through and clearing debris, interrupted every time a small icon of food appears. Keep it light and non-clinical.

### Point 4: The one habit, give your gut a real gap (11:30 to 14:30)

[SPOKEN]: So here is the single easiest change, and I want you to notice what it is not. It is not a new food you have to cut. It is not a new supplement. It is timing.

[SPOKEN]: Give your gut a real gap between meals, somewhere around four to five hours, with nothing but water or plain herbal tea in between. No grazing, no "just a little something" to hold you over, no healthy snack squeezed in at three o'clock. Three real meals, spaced out, with genuine gaps in between, is often enough to let that cleanup crew actually finish its shift instead of getting interrupted over and over.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: EAT MEALS. STOP GRAZING.

[SPOKEN]: And this is why I keep saying this is not a restrictive diet. You are not narrowing what you are allowed to eat. If anything, this usually means you get to eat a wider variety of foods again, because you are addressing the actual location problem instead of removing one more suspect from the list. You are simply changing when you eat, not what.

[SPOKEN]: This is the habit that made the whole video click into place for a lot of my clients, because it is the first thing that actually goes after the leak instead of mopping the floor one more time.

[RETENTION LOOP]: This closes the loop opened in Point 3 (what do I actually do). Land it as the practical payoff of the whole video.
[VISUAL NOTE]: Warm, simple. A clock or a clean, uncluttered plate with clear time markers between meals, not a food-restriction graphic.

### Point 5: How to actually do this without misery (14:30 to 16:45)

[SPOKEN]: A few things make this easy instead of miserable, because "just stop snacking" alone can backfire if you are not set up for it.

[SPOKEN]: One, build real meals around protein and fat, not just carbohydrates, so you are genuinely full for four to five hours instead of white-knuckling hunger. A meal with eggs or chicken, some healthy fat, and vegetables holds you far longer than toast alone. Two, if you truly need something between meals in the beginning, make it water or a plain tea, and give yourself grace, this is a habit you build over a couple of weeks, not a rule you have to nail perfectly on day one. Three, a short walk after a meal can help that cleanup wave along even more, so a ten minute walk after dinner is a nice add-on, not a requirement.

[SPOKEN]: Most women notice less afternoon bloating within the first one to two weeks of giving their gut real gaps between meals. It is not instant, but it tends to be steady, and it keeps working instead of quietly stopping working the way a lot of elimination diets do.

[SPOKEN]: And if you do this consistently and the bloating still is not budging, that is genuinely useful information too. It usually means this pattern runs a little deeper than timing alone can fix, and that is exactly the kind of thing worth looking at more closely with an actual practitioner rather than trying yet another diet on your own.
