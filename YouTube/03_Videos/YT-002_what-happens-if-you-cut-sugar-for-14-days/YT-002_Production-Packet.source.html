Section 1

The Script

Length: 17 to 19 min  ·  Format: solo to camera  ·  One idea: the hardest part of cutting sugar is not willpower, it is that gut microbes have been driving the cravings.
Delivery: conversational and warm. This one is a story, a 14-day timeline, so keep it moving. The small side-notes and colored labels are for editing, do not read them aloud.
0:00 – 0:35Hook

What actually happens inside your body if you cut sugar for fourteen days. Not one day, not a "sugar detox" weekend, a full two weeks. Because the answer is stranger than "you lose a little weight," and most people quit on day three before they ever find out.

On screen: 14 days. No sugar.

I am going to walk you through it day by day, what happens to your cravings, your energy, your bloating, and the part almost nobody talks about, which is what your gut is doing behind the scenes the whole time. And I will tell you the exact day most women say something finally clicks. Stay with me for that one, because it is not the day you would guess.

Editor  Open the "day it clicks" loop now, pay it off at Point 4. B-roll: a calendar, a hand pushing away a dessert, a cup of tea.
0:35 – 1:15Credential moment

Quick hello, because you should know who is walking you through this. I am Alina Nazari, I am a Functional Diagnostic Nutrition Practitioner, and I work with women who feel like sugar has a strange amount of power over them, women who eat well all day and then cannot explain the three o'clock raid on the pantry.

And here is the first thing I want you to hear. If sugar feels like it runs you, that is not a character flaw. By the end of this you are going to understand exactly why, and it is going to take a lot of the shame off the table.

Visual  Warm, direct to camera. Lower third with name and FDN-P appears here.
1:15 – 3:00The 3 o'clock loop

Let me describe the loop, and tell me if this is you. You are good all day. Breakfast is solid, lunch is fine. And then late afternoon hits and something in you goes looking for sugar, and it is not a gentle suggestion, it is loud. So you have the thing, and for twenty minutes you feel better, and then an hour later you are foggy and tired and a little annoyed at yourself, and the whole thing resets the next day.

And the advice you get is always the same. Just have more willpower. Just eat more protein. And those are not wrong exactly, but they miss something huge, because you already have willpower, you run a whole life on willpower, and it still does not touch the three o'clock craving. So either you are uniquely weak, which I promise you are not, or something else is going on.

On screen: It's not a willpower problem

Something else is going on. And a fourteen-day sugar cut is one of the clearest ways to actually see it. So let me show you what happens, and why.

3:00 – 6:30Point 1 · Why the cravings aren't yours

Here is the piece that reframes everything. Your gut is home to trillions of microbes, and they are not just sitting there quietly. They eat what you eat. And some of them, the ones that thrive on sugar and refined carbs, are very good at getting more of their favorite food.

When those sugar-loving microbes are overgrown and running the show, they can actually influence your cravings. They send signals that nudge you toward the exact foods that feed them. Think of it like squatters who moved into your house and figured out how to make you keep stocking the fridge with their favorite snacks. The craving feels like it is coming from you. A lot of it is coming from them.

On screen: The squatters are hungry

This is what I call the Sugar-Driven pattern. And here is the line I want you to remember, because it is the whole video in one sentence.

"The cravings aren't yours. They're something else's, broadcasting through you."

Key line  Deliver this slowly, it reframes the entire experiment. B-roll: playful animation of "squatters" sending out signals. Keep it non-clinical, never imply a diagnosed disease.
6:30 – 9:30Point 2 · Days 1 to 3, where people quit

So now you cut sugar. What happens first? The squatters get loud.

The first three days are usually the hardest, and now you know why. You just stopped delivering the food that a whole population of microbes depends on, and they do not go quietly. This is when the cravings spike, when you might feel irritable, headachy, low energy, a little foggy. People call this a sugar crash or sugar withdrawal, and they assume it means sugar is fine and they need it. It is the opposite. That intensity is the sign that something really was feeding on it.

Here is what I tell my clients so they do not quit at the worst possible moment. The hardest three days are not a warning to turn back. They are the toll you pay at the start. If you can get a plan for these three days, and I will give you one at the end, you get to the good part.

On screen: Days 1 to 3, the toll
Editor  On-screen day counter starts here (Day 1, Day 2, Day 3). Encouraging tone, not scary.
9:30 – 12:30Point 3 · Days 4 to 7, the turn

Somewhere around day four to seven, things shift, and this is the part that surprises people.

Your energy stops doing the roller coaster. Instead of that hard crash in the afternoon, you get a flatter, steadier line, because you are not spiking and dropping your blood sugar every few hours. A lot of women notice they are sleeping a little deeper. And this is often when the bloating starts to settle, because you have stopped feeding the exact microbes that produce a lot of gas when they ferment sugar.

The cravings are still there in this stretch, but they change character. They go from that loud, urgent, must-have-it feeling to something quieter, more like a passing thought you can actually let go of. That change is not you white-knuckling harder. That is the population of squatters starting to shrink because you stopped feeding them.

On screen: Days 4 to 7, the roller coaster flattens
Visual  A jagged blood-sugar line smoothing into a gentle wave. Split screen: a tired afternoon versus a steady one.
12:30 – 15:00Point 4 · Days 8 to 14, the day it clicks

Now the second week, and this is the part I told you to stay for.

The single most common thing women tell me happens somewhere around day ten. It is not a number on a scale. It is a moment. They walk past the thing that used to own them, the candy at the front desk, the pastry with their coffee, and for the first time they realize they genuinely do not want it. Not "I am resisting it." Just, it is not calling anymore. That is the day it clicks.

And it clicks around day ten and not day two for a reason. It takes time to actually change who is living in your gut. The first few days you are just fighting the cravings. By the second week you have changed the environment enough that the cravings themselves quiet down. That is why willpower alone never worked. You were trying to out-discipline a biology problem. When you change the biology, the discipline gets easy.

On screen: The day it stops calling

By day fourteen most people describe feeling clearer, steadier, less bloated, and a little bit free, like they got something back.

Payoff  This closes the loop from the hook. Warm, hopeful. B-roll: a woman walking past a dessert display, unbothered.
15:00 – 16:45Point 5 · How to do it without misery

So if you want to actually try this, here is how to survive the first three days and not quit.

One, do not go hungry. Cutting sugar and cutting calories at the same time is how people fail. Eat plenty of protein, fat, and real, cooked food, so your body is never running on empty. Two, get ahead of the three o'clock craving before it hits, with a real snack in the early afternoon, protein and fat, not a "healthy" sugar swap. Three, expect the first three days to be loud, and treat that as proof it is working, not a reason to stop.

And one important thing. This is not about fearing sugar forever or never having dessert again. It is about taking back the steering wheel so that sugar is a choice you make, not a voice that makes you. After fourteen days, a lot of women find they can have a treat and simply move on, which is the whole point.

If the cravings come roaring right back the second you reintroduce anything, that is useful information too. It usually means the pattern runs a little deeper, and that is exactly the kind of thing worth looking at more closely rather than just trying harder.

Section 4

Shoot Day Checklist & Upload Copy

For Steve. Everything to film it and upload it.

1. Before the shoot

  • Alina reads the script out loud twice. Familiarity, not memorization.
  • Lock the 3 word-for-word lines: the hook, the aha line ("The cravings aren't yours..."), and the close ("You are not weak, and the cravings were never really the whole you.").
  • Wardrobe: solid warm tones (cream, sage, terracotta, navy). No busy patterns, no pure white or black, no big logos.
  • Location: quiet room, soft daylight or a key light, warm non-clinical background.
  • Water nearby, phone silent, notifications off.

2. Gear & setup

  • Camera at eye level, framed chest-up, eyes on the top third.
  • Light in front and slightly to one side, softened. Avoid overhead-only light.
  • Audio is the priority: lav or shotgun mic close to her, not the built-in mic. Record 10 seconds of room tone.
  • Teleprompter app just under or over the lens so her eyeline stays on camera. Slow scroll.
  • Do a 20-second test: focus, exposure, audio levels.

3. Delivery notes for Alina

  • Talk to one person, not an audience (someone who raids the pantry at 3pm).
  • This is a story, a 14-day timeline. Keep it moving, let the day counter carry momentum.
  • Slow down on the hook and the aha line. Pauses read as confidence.
  • Energy slightly higher than feels natural (camera flattens it ~20%).
  • Fine to restart a sentence: pause, clap once (helps the editor), redo the line.
  • Take the shame off the table, that is the emotional job of this video.

4. Shot list

A-roll: film each section as its own take: Hook, Credential, Problem, Points 1 to 5, Bridge/CTA, Outro.

B-roll (10 to 20 sec each, silent):

  • A hand pushing away a dessert or pastry (hook).
  • An afternoon office scene, a candy drawer, coffee with a pastry (problem).
  • A woman walking past a dessert display, unbothered (Point 4 payoff).
  • A simple protein-and-fat afternoon snack being made (Point 5, also a clip).
  • Optional: a calendar with days crossed off, a cup of tea, warm cozy cues.
