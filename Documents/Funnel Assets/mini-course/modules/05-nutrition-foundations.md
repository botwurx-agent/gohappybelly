## Every Meal Is An Input

Your microbiome responds to what you feed it on a timescale of days, not years.
That is good news, because it means the thing you change this week registers.

It also means there is no single correct diet here. What there is: a few
principles that hold for almost everyone, and a lot of individual variation
underneath them that only your own body can tell you about.

## Fiber, In More Detail

Module four gave you the number. Here is what it is actually doing.

Prebiotic fiber is the part of plants you cannot break down. Your bacteria can.
When they ferment it they produce short-chain fatty acids, and those are what
the cells lining your colon use for fuel. Low fiber means underfed colon cells,
which is a quieter problem than constipation and a more consequential one.

**Where to find it:**

- Vegetables: artichokes, broccoli, leafy greens
- Fruit: berries, apples, pears
- Other: flax, chia, oats, lentils, beans

Variety matters more than quantity past a point. Thirty different plants a week
does more for the range of your bacteria than large amounts of the same three.

## Fermented Foods

Sauerkraut, kimchi, kefir, yogurt, miso, kombucha. These bring in live bacteria
and the compounds produced during fermentation.

Start with a spoonful. Not a serving, a spoonful.

**And a real caution.** Fermented foods are not universally good for everyone,
and this is one of the places where generic advice does measurable harm. If you
are in a pattern involving overgrowth or histamine sensitivity, fermented foods
can make you feel distinctly worse, and the usual interpretation of that is "I
am detoxing, I should push through." You are not necessarily detoxing. You may
be feeding something.

If sauerkraut gives you a headache, flushing, or a racing heart, that is
information. Stop and note it.

> **Alina's take.** This is the single most common place I see people go wrong
> on their own. They read that fermented foods are good for the gut, which is
> true in general, and they double down when they feel bad, which is exactly
> backwards. Feeling worse on something is data. It is not a sign you need more
> of it.

## Digestive Enzymes

Your body makes them. They break protein, fat and carbohydrate into usable
pieces. Production falls with age, with stress, and when the stomach is not
doing its part upstream.

Enzyme supplements exist and they help some people considerably. Whether you are
one of them depends on your pattern, which is a conversation rather than a
purchase.

## Mindful Eating

The least impressive heading in this course and one of the most useful items in it.

Chewing properly starts the chemical breakdown and signals the rest of the
system to get ready. Eating in a calm state puts you in the nervous system mode
where digestion actually runs. Eating standing up, in six minutes, between
calls, does the opposite.

You can eat a perfect meal badly and get a fraction of it.

## A Plate That Works

- Half colorful vegetables
- A quarter protein
- A quarter carbohydrate with fiber
- Something fermented, if you tolerate it
