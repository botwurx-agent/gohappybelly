## [BODY]

### Point 1: Your gut has two gears, and only one of them digests (3:00 to 6:00)

[SPOKEN]: Your nervous system has two main settings. One is often called rest and digest. The other is fight or flight. And they are almost exactly what they sound like.

[SPOKEN]: When you are in rest and digest, your body feels safe. Blood flows to your stomach and intestines. You make stomach acid. You make digestive enzymes. The muscles of your gut do this slow, coordinated wave that moves food along and sweeps things clean between meals. Digestion is not a light switch, it is an orchestra, and this is the setting where the orchestra actually plays in time.

[SPOKEN]: Now flip to fight or flight. Your body thinks there is a threat. It does not matter if the threat is a bear or a full inbox, your body cannot tell the difference. So it does something smart for an emergency and terrible for digestion. It pulls resources away from your gut, because digesting lunch is not the priority when you are supposedly running from a bear. Stomach acid drops. Enzymes drop. That clean-up wave between meals slows down or stalls.

[SPOKEN]: This is the analogy I give every client, and it is the one I want you to remember. Think of your digestion like a car engine, and the timing is off. Every part is there. Every part still fires. But they are firing in the wrong sequence, so instead of running smooth, the whole thing sputters, and food that should move through you smoothly just sits there, fermenting, producing gas.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: SAME ENGINE. WRONG TIMING.

[SPOKEN]: The food is fine. The system processing it isn't.

[RETENTION LOOP]: This is the locked aha line, land it slowly. It pays off the hook.

### Point 2: Why "eating cleaner" quietly makes it worse (6:00 to 9:30)

[SPOKEN]: Now here is the trap, and I need you to really hear this one, because it is the thing that keeps smart, disciplined women stuck for years.

[SPOKEN]: When your digestion is stuck in the wrong gear and you bloat, your instinct is to eat cleaner and eat less. But think about what that actually does. Stress already lowered your stomach acid and your enzymes. Then you start eating a giant raw salad and a pile of raw vegetables, because that is what "clean" looks like, and raw, high-fiber food is some of the hardest food to break down.

[SPOKEN]: So now you have a weak digestive setup trying to process the most demanding possible meal, in a body that still feels unsafe. That is not a diet problem you can subtract your way out of. That is a timing problem. And no amount of removing foods fixes the timing.

[SPOKEN]: This is why you can do a perfect elimination diet and still bloat. It is why the bloating seems to have a mind of its own, worse on your most stressful days, weirdly fine on vacation on day four when your shoulders finally drop. You have probably noticed that. ### Point 3: What chronic stress does over months, not minutes (9:30 to 12:30)

[SPOKEN]: So far I have described what happens in a stressful moment. But most of you are not dealing with a moment. You are dealing with years. And that changes things.

[SPOKEN]: When your body sits in that low-grade emergency mode for months and years, a few things tend to stack up. That clean-up wave between meals, the one that sweeps your small intestine clean, it keeps getting interrupted, so things that should keep moving start to linger and settle where they should not. Your body is producing less of the acid and enzymes that would normally keep everything in check. And your gut lining, which is doing a lot of quiet work at the best of times, is trying to do that work in a body that keeps telling it there is an emergency.

[SPOKEN]: I am not going to name a condition here, because you do not need a scary label, you need the pattern. And the pattern is this. It is rarely one dramatic thing. It is a system that has been asked to digest under pressure for so long that it forgot how to shift back into the calm gear on its own. That is what I call the Wired-and-Tired pattern. Wired, because your system is revved up. Tired, because running an engine in the wrong gear all day is exhausting, and I bet you are exhausted.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: WIRED. AND TIRED.

[VISUAL NOTE]: Calm, do not fear-monger. Alina's tone here is reassuring.

### Point 4: The signs your bloating is stress-driven (12:30 to 15:00)

[SPOKEN]: So how do you know if this is you? Here are the signs I look for, and this is the part I asked you to stay for.

[SPOKEN]: One. Your bloating is worse in the afternoon and evening, and better first thing in the morning before the day gets to you.

[SPOKEN]: Two. It tracks your stress more than any single food. Big deadline week, worse. Slow weekend, better.

[SPOKEN]: Three. You eat fast, standing up, between meetings, without really tasting it. Eating in a rush keeps you in the wrong gear before the food even lands.

[SPOKEN]: Four. You are tired but wired. You crash in the afternoon, then get a second wind at night when you finally want to sleep.

[SPOKEN]: And here is the fifth one, the sign almost everyone misses. You feel better when you eat cooked, warm, simple food, and worse when you eat raw and cold and complicated, even when the raw food is technically "healthier." If a warm bowl of soup sits better than a big raw salad, your body is telling you the issue is capacity, not the food. That one sign reframes everything.

### Point 5: The simplest place to actually start (15:00 to 16:45)

[SPOKEN]: So what do you do with all this? Not another elimination diet, I promise. You start by changing the gear you eat in, before you ever change what is on the plate.

[SPOKEN]: The simplest, most underrated thing you can do is this. Before you eat, take sixty seconds. Sit down, actually sit, put your fork down, and take five slow breaths where the exhale is longer than the inhale. That long exhale is a direct signal to your nervous system that you are safe, and it nudges you toward the digest gear before the first bite. It sounds too simple to matter. It is not. You are not adding a supplement, you are changing the setting the whole meal runs in.

[SPOKEN]: Then, favor warm, cooked, simple meals while your system relearns the calm gear, especially earlier in the day. You are not doing this forever. You are giving a tired system an easier job while it recovers.

[SPOKEN]: None of this is about willpower, and none of it is about you being broken. It is about giving the orchestra a chance to play in time again.
