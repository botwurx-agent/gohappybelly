## A Line That Runs Both Ways

Your brain and your gut are connected by the vagus nerve, and most of the
traffic on it goes upward. Your gut reports to your brain more than your brain
instructs your gut.

Which means two things. Stress changes digestion, and digestion changes mood.
Neither is the junior partner.

## Rest And Digest, Or Fight And Flight

Your nervous system runs in modes, and digestion only happens properly in one
of them.

In rest-and-digest, stomach acid is produced, enzymes are released, the muscular
movement that carries food along runs normally. In fight-or-flight, your body
makes a reasonable decision: something urgent is happening, and digesting lunch
is not the priority. Acid drops. Movement slows or speeds up. Blood goes
elsewhere.

That system is excellent at what it was built for. Its weakness is that it
cannot distinguish a genuine emergency from a difficult email, a running late,
a hard conversation, a year of low-grade worry. So for a great many people it is
on far more of the day than it was designed to be.

## What That Does Downstream

- Less stomach acid, so protein is broken down less completely
- Fewer enzymes released
- Movement slowed, so things sit
- A barrier under more strain

None of that is exotic. It is the ordinary consequence of digesting in the wrong
mode, repeated daily for years.

> **Alina's take.** This is the pattern I see most in women who are doing
> everything right. Clean food, good sleep on paper, exercise, supplements. And
> still reacting to foods that should be fine.
>
> It is a car with the engine timing off. Every part works. They are firing in
> the wrong sequence, and you cannot fix timing by putting in better fuel.
>
> **The food is fine. The system processing it isn't.**

## What Actually Shifts It

The useful interventions here are unglamorous and they work because they signal
safety rather than because they do anything to the gut directly.

- **Three slow breaths before eating.** Longer out than in. Twenty seconds
- **A ten-minute walk after a meal**
- **Eating sitting down**, not standing, not driving
- **Gratitude, prayer, music, time outdoors**, whichever of those is genuinely
  yours rather than the one that sounds right

## A Practice You Can Do Now

In for four. Hold for four. Out for six.

Three rounds. The longer exhale is the part that matters, because that is what
tips you toward the mode where digestion runs. Do it before your next meal.

## When It Is Worth Looking Further

Persistent fatigue, sleep that does not restore, anxiety alongside digestive
symptoms, reacting to more foods over time. Functional testing can look at
cortisol rhythm, which is one of the few ways to see this pattern rather than
infer it.
