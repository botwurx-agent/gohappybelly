## When The Problem Is Not The Food

Module six covered the gut-brain line. This module is for the person for whom
that line is the whole story.

You know who you are. The food is good. The supplements are taken. The sleep is
attempted. And your digestion is still unreliable in a way that tracks with how
your week is going rather than with what is on your plate.

**The food is fine. The system processing it isn't.**

## What Chronic Pressure Actually Does

Not metaphorically. Mechanically:

- **Stomach acid drops.** Protein is broken down less completely, and the
  checkpoint that acid provides gets weaker
- **Fewer enzymes** are released
- **Movement changes.** For some people it slows and things sit. For others it
  speeds up and nothing is absorbed properly. Which direction you go is fairly
  consistent per person
- **The barrier comes under strain**

## Three Modes, Three Kinds Of Bad Digestion

Most people think stress has one setting. It has at least three, and they
produce different symptoms.

| Mode | What digestion does |
|---|---|
| **Fight** | Tight, reactive. Cramping. Reacting to foods that are usually fine |
| **Flight** | Fast. Urgency. Things move through before much is absorbed |
| **Freeze** | Slow and heavy. Fatigue. Nothing moving, no appetite |

Knowing which one is yours is more useful than knowing you are stressed. The
freeze pattern in particular gets misread as laziness or low motivation, by
other people and by the person in it.

## What Stress Spends

Chronic pressure draws down the things digestion needs:

- Magnesium
- B vitamins
- Zinc
- Electrolytes
- Short-chain fatty acids, the fuel your colon lining runs on

So it is a double hit. You absorb less well at exactly the time you need more.

## Two Ways People Eat Under Stress

Most people are one or the other, consistently.

**Stress eaters** reach for food that is fast and sweet, often late, often
standing up. The eating is real but the digesting barely happens, because of
the mode they are in while doing it.

**Stress skippers** forget entirely, run on coffee until two in the afternoon,
then eat a large meal into a system that has been idle all day and is not ready
for it.

Neither is a willpower failure. Both are what the nervous system does when it
has decided there is something more urgent than lunch. But they need different
corrections, so it is worth knowing which you are.

## Why Everything Suddenly Bothers You

Under sustained pressure, tolerance drops across the board: caffeine, alcohol,
large meals, fried food, sugar, and the fermentable carbohydrates in a lot of
otherwise healthy vegetables.

This is the thing that sends people down the elimination path. The foods look
like the problem because removing them helps. But they are not new offenders.
Your capacity changed. Which is why the list keeps growing no matter how much
you cut, and why cutting is not the way out.

> **Alina's take.** It is a car with the engine timing off. Every part works.
> They are firing in the wrong sequence, and you cannot fix timing by putting in
> better fuel.
>
> I have had women arrive with twelve foods eliminated and no more comfort than
> when they had two. The list is not the answer. The sequence is.

## What Actually Works For Someone With No Time

The advice usually given to stressed people assumes they have an hour and a
yoga mat. They do not. These take minutes and they work because they change the
mode, not because they do anything to the gut directly.

- **Three slow breaths before eating.** Longer out than in. Twenty seconds
- **Eat every three to four hours.** Steadiness beats optimization
- **Ten-minute walks.** After meals ideally, but any walk
- **Water first thing**, before coffee
- **Downshift the evening.** Whatever that means for you, done at the same time
  most nights

## What To Prioritize Nutritionally

Magnesium, omega-3s, B vitamins, electrolytes, protein with fiber, and
polyphenols from colorful plants, coffee, tea, olive oil and cocoa.

Food first. Supplements on top of a pattern you understand, not instead of one.

## Stress And Your Bacteria

Sustained stress shifts the population: more of the inflammatory kinds, fewer
of the beneficial ones, less of the short-chain fatty acid production your colon
depends on.

Worth saying plainly, because it means the stress effect is not only about the
moment you are eating in. It compounds.

## When To Look Deeper

If several of these are true at once, general support is probably not enough:

- Daily bloating
- Fatigue that sleep does not resolve
- Constipation or urgency that has become the norm
- Insomnia
- Anxiety alongside digestive symptoms
- Crashes after meals
- Reacting to more foods over time
- Needing more caffeine, or more alcohol to come down

Functional testing can look at cortisol rhythm, which is one of the few ways to
see this pattern rather than guess at it.
