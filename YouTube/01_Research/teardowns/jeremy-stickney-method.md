# Method Teardown: Jeremy Stickney (and the Evan Carmichael system he descends from)

- **Who:** Jeremy Stickney, YouTube growth consultant. Publicly described as Evan Carmichael's "right hand man and business partner." He started working for Carmichael for free while still working a grocery-store job, and within six months the two had built a YouTube consulting business together (Backstage Careers podcast). He has since consulted creators outside the Carmichael orbit directly, including a recorded interview for a yoga/wellness-teacher training course (Yoga With Kassandra's "YouTube 101 for Yoga Teachers & Health Professionals"), showing his method transfers to health/wellness niches, not just business/entrepreneurship content.
- **Track record claimed:** growing 2,000,000+ subscribers, documented in a talk titled "What Growing 2,000,000 Subscribers Taught Me About Building A Successful YouTube Channel" (released as an Audible Original and as an episode of The Passionate Few podcast, September 2020).
- **Why his method matters for this project:** Ben Azadi (Keto Kamp, 1.32M subscribers, the anchor channel for this research track, see `ben-azadi.md`) credits much of his channel's growth to Jeremy. Jeremy's specific coaching sessions with Ben are not public. What is public is (a) the confirmed fact of the Carmichael-to-Stickney lineage, and (b) an extensively documented Evan Carmichael system that Jeremy was trained inside and co-built a consulting business around. The working assumption for this project, stated plainly as an assumption: Jeremy's method is a refinement of Carmichael's documented system, applied at Keto Kamp. The pattern-match between Ben Azadi's actual channel behavior (near-daily uploads, solo single-topic videos outperforming long guest interviews, heavy thumbnail/title consistency, see `ben-azadi.md`) and the Carmichael principles below supports that assumption, but it is inference, not a confirmed direct transcript of Jeremy coaching Ben.

## Documented Method Principles

Each principle below is phrased as something Alina's channel can act on directly. Attribution and confidence are marked per item.

1. **Model proven outlier videos instead of guessing at topics.** Find videos that are outperforming their channel's normal subscriber-to-view ratio (an "outlier"), then make an Alina-original video on the same topic with her own angle and a similar (not copied) title. Carmichael's own language for this: "success leaves clues." *(Documented, Carmichael system, erikavieira.net, marketingspeak.com)*

2. **Write titles by studying what already works, not by guessing.** Research high-performing titles in the niche and reverse-engineer the structure (the emotional trigger, the specific-number claim, the curiosity gap) rather than freehand copywriting. Carmichael has specifically pointed to studying lifestyle-magazine headlines for word choice and emotional pull. *(Documented, Carmichael system, erikavieira.net, liveyourmessage.com)*

3. **Treat YouTube's recommendation engine as the primary growth lever, search as secondary.** Carmichael: "Most views on YouTube don't come from search. Most views on YouTube come from the recommendation engine." The practical implication: winning is about getting placed next to (or after) an existing successful video in suggested/up-next, which is why matching an outlier's topic and title structure matters more than keyword-stuffing. Other Carmichael-adjacent sources describe YouTube as also functioning as a search/problem-solving engine for evergreen, question-based content, so both channels of discovery are worth designing for, with recommendation weighted as primary. *(Documented, Carmichael system, with a noted tension across sources, marketingspeak.com vs. ilovemarketing.com)*

4. **Consistency beats polish. Post more, not better, up to a point.** Carmichael: "80% of YouTube success is making daily content that is fire," and he has explicitly recommended posting daily at 80% quality over posting weekly at 100% quality, with a floor of 1-3 long-form uploads per week when daily isn't realistic. *(Documented, Carmichael system, erikavieira.net, liveyourmessage.com)*

5. **Hook in the first seconds like it's an ad for the rest of the video.** Carmichael: "think of the start of your video like it's an advertisement for everything after that." A separate source frames this as opening on a compelling opinion or perspective rather than a greeting or pleasantries. *(Documented, Carmichael system, liveyourmessage.com, ilovemarketing.com)*

6. **Favor long-form (10-20+ minutes, sometimes much longer) over short, thin videos, because supply is scarce relative to demand.** Carmichael's stated minimum is 10 minutes, with a stated preference for going much longer when there's real substance, on the logic that YouTube is a platform people go to for learning, not quick entertainment, and long-form performs better there. This is specifically about long-form uploads, not Shorts, which are a separate, additive distribution layer, not a replacement. *(Documented, Carmichael system, brandbuildersgroup.com, liveyourmessage.com)*

7. **Build a consistent visual identity so the channel is instantly recognizable in a suggested-videos grid.** Consistent thumbnail color palette, consistent framing, and a repeated visual "look" trains viewers to recognize and click on the channel's videos without reading the title. *(Documented, Carmichael system, marketingspeak.com, erikavieira.net)*

8. **Design thumbnails for a small, mobile screen, not for looking good full-size.** Documented specifics: bold, large, high-contrast text; close-up faces or images that trigger curiosity; avoid black-and-white or low-contrast images. Carmichael has also reported that raw, unpolished screencap-style thumbnails outperformed polished graphic-design thumbnails across testing, and that a thumbnail swap alone produced a 300%+ click-through increase on an old video. *(Documented, Carmichael system, liveyourmessage.com, marketingspeak.com)*

9. **Track audience retention, and treat the retention curve as directional data, not just a vanity stat.** A documented target is roughly 70% retention at the one-minute mark. Spikes and rewatched sections in the retention curve mark the strongest moments in a video, and those are the segments to pull out and repurpose. *(Documented, Carmichael system, marketingspeak.com, brandbuildersgroup.com)*

10. **Cut every long-form upload into multiple shorter pieces for other platforms, chosen from the highest-retention segments.** One long-form video becomes source material for Shorts, Reels, and TikTok clips, prioritizing the moments the retention curve shows people rewatched or leaned into. *(Documented, Carmichael system, brandbuildersgroup.com, liveyourmessage.com)*

11. **Direct viewers to a specific next video at the end of every video, not just a generic subscribe ask.** Use end screens and pinned comments that link to a related video or playlist, so a single viewer session chains into multiple views. *(Documented, Carmichael system, marketingspeak.com, erikavieira.net)*

12. **Reply to comments.** Carmichael-linked sources report viewers who get an on-camera-creator reply are roughly seven times more likely to subscribe. *(Documented, Carmichael system, marketingspeak.com)*

13. **Lead every video with education, not a pitch, and let the expertise itself build the case for the paid offer.** "Put education in front of the sale. You have to earn the right to sell something to somebody, you have to earn their trust." Content should map to what the channel ultimately sells (its offer suite), without being an ad for it. *(Documented, Carmichael system, brandbuildersgroup.com, ilovemarketing.com)*

14. **Play to personal strengths and delegate or cut the parts of production that aren't a strength**, so more usable volume gets made per week. Carmichael has framed this as "being you" resonating more than a highly polished, over-produced video. *(Documented, Carmichael system, erikavieira.net, liveyourmessage.com)*

15. **Batch-record multiple videos in a single sitting** to sustain a high, consistent upload cadence without needing a new setup/mindset every session. *(Documented, Carmichael system, ilovemarketing.com)*

16. **Repurpose recorded 1:1 coaching or client-question sessions into public content.** Carmichael-linked sources describe this as a way to generate an ongoing, low-effort content stream directly from real client conversations, framed as "education at scale." *(Documented, Carmichael system, brandbuildersgroup.com)* Not directly applicable to Go Happy Belly without client consent and without violating client privacy; noted here for completeness, flagged as requiring adaptation, see below.

## Carmichael-System Lineage (clearly attributed)

Everything in the numbered list above is attributed to the Evan Carmichael system specifically, sourced from public interviews, podcast recaps, and Carmichael's own "YouTube Growth Bootcamp" sales materials, not from a direct Jeremy Stickney transcript. The lineage claim itself, that Jeremy's method descends from this system, rests on two confirmed public facts:

- Jeremy worked directly under Evan Carmichael and co-built a YouTube consulting business with him within six months of starting (Backstage Careers podcast episode title: "How This 24-Year-Old Makes $50,000/Month As A YouTube Consultant").
- Carmichael's own foundational philosophy, predating the YouTube-specific tactics, is "Modeling the Masters," the practice of studying successful people's documented strategies and reapplying them, which is the same modeling logic embedded in tactic #1 above (model proven outlier videos). Carmichael's YouTube system is, by his own framing, an application of his general modeling philosophy to the platform itself.

Additional Carmichael Bootcamp-specific tactics (from the program's own sales page, so read as marketing claims rather than independently verified case studies, but included because they are concrete and plausible extensions of the principles above):
- A "Suggested Views" strategy aimed at getting a video recommended alongside larger channels' videos without paid ads.
- A "Bridge Audience" technique using small paid YouTube ad spend to guarantee placement next to a competitor's videos, distinct from and smaller-scale than a general paid-ads strategy.
- Systematic thumbnail split-testing as an ongoing practice, not a one-time decision.

*(Source for this block: believe.evancarmichael.com/gb-sales, treat as vendor marketing copy, useful for directional ideas, not verified data.)*

## How This Applies to Go Happy Belly

Translated into non-diagnostic, pattern/archetype-safe guidance. No principle below authorizes diagnostic language; "your pattern suggests," "this often shows up when," and the five locked archetype analogies stay in force throughout.

- **Modeling outliers (#1, #2):** When planning topics, look at what is overperforming on channels like Ben Azadi's (see `ben-azadi.md` outlier list) and in the broader gut-health/functional-health space, then build an Alina-original video on the same underlying question, filtered through one of the five gut archetypes and always framed as pattern, never diagnosis. Example: Ben's "brands to avoid" format translates cleanly into a "products that may aggravate a Reactive Gut pattern" video.
- **Recommendation-first, search-secondary (#3):** Title and package videos to sit naturally next to existing high-performing gut-health and functional-health content (so YouTube recommends Alina's video after or alongside those), while still including the plain-language search terms a symptomatic viewer would type (e.g., "why am I bloated after eating," "always tired gut").
- **Daily-content mindset (#4):** Given Alina does not personally run the channel, the practical version of "consistency beats polish" is a repeatable weekly production system (this pipeline) that reliably outputs 1-3 long-form videos a week plus daily-cadence Shorts cut from that long-form, rather than waiting for a "perfect" video.
- **Hook as an ad for the video (#5):** Every script should open with the exact symptom or frustration the target avatar is feeling (e.g., Avatar 1, The Dismissed Patient: "your labs came back normal, and you still feel awful") before any explanation, mirroring Carmichael's "advertisement for everything after it" framing.
- **Long-form preference (#6):** Default to 12-20 minute solo, single-topic videos, consistent with what already works on Ben Azadi's channel (see `ben-azadi.md` cadence notes), not long guest-interview formats, at least while the channel is building watch history.
- **Visual consistency and mobile-first thumbnails (#7, #8):** Lock a repeatable thumbnail system for Alina (consistent color palette in the brand's navy/sage/terracotta/cream system, close-up expressive face, large bold text, 2-4 words) so the channel is recognizable in a suggested-videos grid, the same way Ben Azadi's face-plus-bold-caps format is instantly recognizable on his channel.
- **Retention-driven clipping (#9, #10):** Every long-form video should be reviewed for its highest-retention segment and cut into 2-4 short pieces for Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts, prioritizing the moment where the "aha line" for that video's archetype lands (e.g., "The foods aren't the problem. The barrier is.").
- **End-of-video chaining and comment replies (#11, #12):** Every video should point to one specific next video (ideally the next archetype in the series, or the quiz), and comments should get real replies, since this is a documented lift to subscribe rate.
- **Education before the pitch (#13):** Every video should fully answer the question in its title before any mention of the Gut Breakthrough Call, the quiz, or Guthub, consistent with the existing funnel architecture (organic content leads to quiz leads to call, not content-as-ad).
- **Play to strengths (#14) and repurposed coaching content (#16):** If Alina is not the on-camera presence for every video, structure around whatever format plays to her actual strengths (e.g., short expert-answer clips versus scripted long-form) rather than forcing a mismatched format. Any use of real client material as content source (#16) requires explicit client consent and full de-identification; this is a hard requirement, not optional, and is flagged here specifically because the sourced Carmichael tactic assumes a business-coaching context where that consent dynamic is different from a health-practice context.
