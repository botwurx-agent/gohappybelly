## Notes on the wording

**"Eleven lessons tell you what is going on."** Ties the bump to the thing in
the cart rather than pitching cold. The buyer has just read "eleven lessons" on
the sales page and in the stat bar, so the phrase is already in their head.

**"the patterns show up on paper"** is Alina's register: an everyday image doing
the work of an abstract claim. It also quietly explains *why* a log is worth
$10, which a bare feature list would not.

**"the foods that commonly sit behind them"** is deliberate. "Commonly" keeps it
in pattern language. It does not tell the reader which foods are her problem,
which would be diagnosing her.

**The refund line is the close, not a disclaimer.** At $10 the objection is not
price, it is "will this be another thing I buy and never use." The guarantee
already exists on the product, so stating it costs nothing and removes the only
real hesitation.

**No urgency, no fake discount.** The Gut Fix is $10 whether bought here or on
its own, so none of this copy implies a saving. Writing "normally $10, yours
here for $10" would be the kind of small dishonesty that costs more than the
bump earns with this audience.

Compliance: pattern language, nothing diagnostic, no claim to heal, seal, cure
or treat, no em dashes, none of the banned words.
