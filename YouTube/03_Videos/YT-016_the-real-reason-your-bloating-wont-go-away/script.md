### Point 1: It's not a food, it's a neighborhood (3:00 to 6:00)

[SPOKEN]: Your gut is supposed to work like a house with very clear rooms. Your large intestine is where most of your bacteria are meant to live, in large numbers, doing useful work. Your small intestine, right before it, is supposed to stay relatively quiet, mostly bacteria-free, because that is where you actually absorb your nutrients.

[SPOKEN]: For a lot of women dealing with bloating that will not quit, some of those bacteria have relocated. Not new bacteria, not bad bacteria, the same bacteria that are supposed to be there, just living in the wrong room. When they end up in the small intestine in bigger numbers than they should, they start fermenting food earlier than they are supposed to, right where you are trying to absorb it. That fermentation makes gas. The gas makes you bloat, often within twenty, thirty minutes of eating, sometimes so much you look pregnant by the end of the day.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: WRONG ROOM, NOT WRONG FOOD

[SPOKEN]: The bacteria aren't the problem. The location is.

[SPOKEN]: So if the mop was never going to fix the leak, what does?

[SPOKEN]: First, spacing. You give real space between meals, four to five hours, instead of grazing all day, because your gut has a natural cleaning wave between meals that sweeps leftover material through, and that wave barely gets a chance to run if you are never actually done eating.
