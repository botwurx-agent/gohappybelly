The Script

Length: 17 to 19 min  ·  Format: solo to camera  ·  One idea: recurring bloating is not about a food you have not found yet, it is bacteria living in the wrong part of your gut, and the 14-day reset addresses that, not another list of foods to cut.
Delivery: conversational, warm, direct. This one carries more validation weight than usual, talk to the woman who has already done the "right" things and is still bloated. The small side-notes and colored labels are for editing, do not read them aloud. The 14-day reset described here is educational, something Alina teaches, not a personal challenge she is running on camera, so this is shoot-ready now.
0:00 – 0:35Hook

You have done the elimination diet. You cut gluten, then dairy, then half the vegetables in your kitchen. You took the probiotic. For a week or two it got better. And then, without changing anything, the bloating came back. Same as before, sometimes worse.

Here is the real reason that happens, and it is not that you missed a trigger food. In the next few minutes I am going to show you why bloating relapses even when you did everything right, the specific reason it keeps returning that almost nobody explains to you, and the simple two-week reset that actually addresses it instead of just managing around it. Stay with me, because the reason it comes back is not what you think, and once you see it you will stop blaming yourself for "falling off" a diet that was never going to fix this in the first place.

1:15 – 3:00The relapse cycle

Let me describe the cycle, and tell me if this is you. You get bloated, so you assume it is something you ate. You start cutting. First the obvious things, then the less obvious things, until your "safe list" is smaller than what you actually eat in a normal day. You feel a little better for a while. Then, with no clear trigger, it comes right back, and now you are more restricted than before and still bloated.

And here is what that does to a person over time. You start doubting your own body. You start wondering if you are just sensitive to everything, or worse, if you are the problem. Meanwhile every test your doctor ran comes back "normal," which somehow makes it feel even more confusing, because normal labs are supposed to mean you are fine, and you very much do not feel fine.

On screen: Normal labs. Still bloated.

Here is what I want you to understand before we go any further. The fact that your labs are normal and you still feel this way does not mean nothing is wrong. It means the standard panel was never designed to look at the thing that is actually happening. Let me show you what that thing is.

3:00 – 6:00Point 1 · It's not a food, it's a neighborhood

Your gut is supposed to work like a house with very clear rooms. Your large intestine is where most of your bacteria are meant to live, in large numbers, doing useful work. Your small intestine, right before it, is supposed to stay relatively quiet, mostly bacteria-free, because that is where you actually absorb your nutrients.

For a lot of women dealing with bloating that will not quit, some of those bacteria have relocated. Not new bacteria, not bad bacteria, the same bacteria that are supposed to be there, just living in the wrong room. When they end up in the small intestine in bigger numbers than they should, they start fermenting food earlier than they are supposed to, right where you are trying to absorb it. That fermentation makes gas. The gas makes you bloat, often within twenty, thirty minutes of eating, sometimes so much you look pregnant by the end of the day.

On screen: Wrong room, not wrong food

This is what I call the Bloated-and-Backed-Up pattern, bacteria that moved to the wrong neighborhood. And here is the line I want you to take with you, because it explains why cutting foods never fully worked.

"The bacteria aren't the problem. The location is."

Key line  Deliver this slowly, it reframes the whole video. B-roll: simple non-clinical animation of a house floor plan, a room labeled correctly versus a room with figures that do not belong there, moving in.
6:00 – 9:00Point 2 · Why it always comes back, the flooded basement

Now here is the part that answers the question I opened with, why the bloating comes back even after you did the diet right.

Cutting certain foods can genuinely calm things down for a while, because you are giving those relocated bacteria less to ferment. But think about what you are actually doing. You are managing the water on the floor. You are not fixing the leak that keeps letting the water back in.

Picture a basement that keeps flooding. You can mop it up every single time, and the floor looks dry for a few days. But if there is an actual leak somewhere, a crack, a pipe, something letting water back in, the basement floods again. Mopping never was going to fix a leak. Cutting foods is the mop. It never touches the reason bacteria keep drifting back into the wrong room in the first place.

On screen: You've been mopping the floor

That is the relapse you have felt. You did not fail the diet. The diet was never designed to reach the leak. This is the recurrence problem, and it is the single biggest reason women tell me they have "tried everything" and nothing sticks.

Payoff  This closes the loop from the hook. B-roll: a basement floor with a little water, someone mopping, then the water slowly seeping back in from a small crack. Keep it clearly metaphorical, not a medical illustration.
9:00 – 11:15Point 3 · Why the standard labs missed it

I promised I would show you why the normal labs did not catch this, so let me actually explain it, because you deserve a real answer, not "your labs are fine, must be stress."

A standard blood panel looks at things like your complete blood count, your basic metabolic markers, maybe your thyroid. Useful information, but none of it is designed to ask "where exactly are your bacteria living, and is fermentation happening earlier in your digestive tract than it should." That is a completely different question, and it requires a completely different kind of test to even ask it.

So when your doctor says your labs are normal, they are telling you the truth about the labs they ran. They are just not telling you those labs were never aimed at this pattern in the first place. Normal results and a real, physical pattern are not contradictions. They are just two different maps of two different territories.

11:15 – 13:45Point 4 · The signs this pattern is yours

So how do you know if this is actually what is going on with you? Here are the signs I look for.

One. The bloating shows up fast, often within thirty to sixty minutes of eating, and it gets visibly worse as the day goes on, flatter in the morning, distended by evening.

Two. Certain healthy foods make it worse, not better, especially beans, onions, garlic, certain fruits, the exact foods that get labeled "gut healthy" everywhere online. That is often a clue you are dealing with fermentation happening in the wrong place, not a food that is bad for you in general.

Three. It came on or got dramatically worse after a round of food poisoning, a course of antibiotics, or a stretch of high stress, any of the things that can disrupt the normal cleanup rhythm of your gut.

Four. You have tried an elimination diet, felt better briefly, and relapsed with no clear new trigger, exactly the pattern we already walked through.

And five, the one almost nobody connects. You often feel worse on probiotics, not better, at least at first. If adding "good bacteria" made your bloating spike, that is not you being broken, that is a strong clue this is a location issue, not a not-enough-good-bacteria issue.

On screen: Probiotics made it worse? That's a clue.

13:45 – 16:45Point 5 · The 14-day reset that actually addresses it

So if the mop was never going to fix the leak, what does?

This is where I want to introduce something I teach my clients, a simple 14-day reset built to give your system's natural cleanup rhythm a real chance to work again, instead of just avoiding foods forever. I want to be clear about what this is. It is not a permanent restriction. It is a short, structured window, and here is what it focuses on.

First, spacing. You give real space between meals, four to five hours, instead of grazing all day, because your gut has a natural cleaning wave between meals that sweeps leftover material through, and that wave barely gets a chance to run if you are never actually done eating.

Second, a temporary lower-fermentation way of eating for those fourteen days specifically, not because those foods are bad, but because giving the wrong-room fermentation a break for two weeks is part of how the reset works. This is short-term and strategic, not a forever list.

Third, supporting your body's own digestive rhythm, things like eating without rushing, chewing fully, and giving your body cues that it is safe to run its normal maintenance in between meals.

Fourteen days is not random. It is enough time to interrupt the cycle and let your system's own cleanup process catch up, without turning into another permanent, exhausting list of foods you can never eat again. Most women tell me the bloating noticeably eases somewhere in that second week, which makes sense, because you are not managing symptoms anymore, you are letting the actual system reset.

And I want to say this clearly. Sometimes fourteen days is not enough on its own, and that is not a failure either. If the pattern keeps returning after a reset like this, that is useful information, and it is worth looking at more closely with someone who can actually see where the recurrence is coming from, rather than trying yet another diet by yourself.

Shoot Day Checklist & Upload Copy

For Steve. Everything to film it and upload it.

1. Before the shoot

  • Alina reads the script out loud twice. Familiarity, not memorization.
  • Lock the 3 word-for-word lines: the hook, the aha line ("The bacteria aren't the problem. The location is."), and the close ("You did not fail the diet, and you are not the problem. The location was.").
  • Wardrobe: solid warm tones (cream, sage, terracotta, navy). No busy patterns, no pure white or black, no big logos.
  • Location: quiet room, soft daylight or a key light, warm non-clinical background.
  • Water nearby, phone silent, notifications off.

2. Gear & setup

  • Camera at eye level, framed chest-up, eyes on the top third.
  • Light in front and slightly to one side, softened. Avoid overhead-only light.
  • Audio is the priority: lav or shotgun mic close to her, not the built-in mic. Record 10 seconds of room tone.
  • Teleprompter app just under or over the lens so her eyeline stays on camera. Slow scroll.
  • Do a 20-second test: focus, exposure, audio levels.

3. Delivery notes for Alina

  • Talk to one person, not an audience (someone who already tried the elimination diet and is still bloated).
  • This video carries more validation weight than usual. Let the warmth sit before the direct lines land.
  • Slow down on the hook, the aha line, and the "normal labs, wrong map" line. Pauses read as confidence.
  • Energy slightly higher than feels natural (camera flattens it ~20%).
  • Fine to restart a sentence: pause, clap once (helps the editor), redo the line.
  • The emotional job of this video is taking the self-blame off the table, she did not fail the diet.
  • The 14-day reset here is educational content Alina teaches, not a personal challenge she has to run first. Shoot-ready now, no BTS needed.

4. Shot list

A-roll: film each section as its own take: Hook, Credential, Problem, Points 1 to 5, Bridge/CTA, Outro.

B-roll (10 to 20 sec each, silent):

  • A fridge with a "safe foods" list, a hand closing a food journal (hook/problem).
  • A woman pressing on her stomach at a desk, a lab printout with "normal range" highlighted (problem).
  • A basement floor with a little water on it, someone mopping (Point 2, also a clip).
  • A simple plated meal with a clock visibly in frame, suggesting spacing between meals (Point 5, also a clip).
  • Optional: the "wrong room" floor-plan idea and the leak/basement idea as simple animations added in post, no need to film live.
