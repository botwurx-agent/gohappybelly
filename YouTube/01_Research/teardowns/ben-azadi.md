## Cadence & Format
- Upload frequency: roughly daily. Gaps between the most recent uploads run 17 hours, 1 day, 2 days, 3 days, 5 days, 7 days, 8 days, 9 days, 10 days, 12 days, consistent with a high-volume batch-and-schedule operation. The channel has approximately 3,000 total videos.
- Long-form length range: two distinct formats coexist. (1) Solo educational videos, roughly 16 to 27 minutes, make up the majority of uploads and are the high performers. (2) Long-form guest interviews, roughly 50 to 63 minutes, feature named experts (Dave Asprey, a Peter Attia reference, Izabella Wentz, Kiran Krishnan) and consistently underperform, landing at 6.9K to 13K views even at 2 to 4 weeks old, versus 50K to 500K+ for solo uploads in the same window.
- Shorts usage: not captured in this data pull. The Videos tab sorts used (Popular, Latest) did not surface a separate Shorts shelf, so no conclusion can be drawn either way.

## Hook Patterns (first 15-30 sec of top videos)
No video transcripts were captured in this data pull (only titles, view counts, ages, and general thumbnail observations were pulled), so the patterns below are informed inference from title structure and this creator's title formulas, not directly observed dialogue. They should be treated as a starting hypothesis to test, not a confirmed observation.
- Curiosity-gap titles likely open by restating the exact question on camera before any explanation (a video titled "What Happens When You Stop Eating For 36 Hours" plausibly opens with that same question asked directly to camera)
- Personal-experiment titles likely open with the premise and an immediate anomaly or twist ("I did X for Y days, and by day 3 something happened I didn't expect"), letting the unexpected result be the hook rather than the topic itself
- Authority-hijack videos likely front-load the credential before the claim ("Harvard Doctor," "this heart surgeon has performed 5,000 surgeries") to hook on trust before content
- News-jacking videos likely open by naming the news event directly (RFK Jr, an FDA recall) to hook on relevance and urgency before pivoting to the health lesson

## Gut-Health Adaptation Notes (how Alina applies this, non-diagnostic)
- **Cadence and format lesson first:** the single clearest signal in this data is that Ben's solo 16-27 minute educational videos massively outperform his 50-63 minute guest interviews (6.9K-13K views versus 50K-500K+). Alina's own long-form should default to solo, punchy, single-topic videos rather than long guest podcasts, at least while she is building watch history and channel authority.
- **Wired-and-Tired Gut (HPA axis / nervous system pattern):** adapt Ben's cortisol and sleep content ("Always Waking Up at Night, 1 TBSP Helps Lower Cortisol Naturally," "The Uncomfortable Truth About Cortisol & Insulin Nobody Tells You") into a video built around the Wired-and-Tired pattern and its locked aha line, "The food is fine. The system processing it isn't." Frame around stress and sleep disruption showing up as digestive symptoms, never as a diagnosis.
- **Reactive Gut (inflammation / permeability pattern):** adapt Ben's "brands to avoid" format and his leaky-gut-adjacent supplement content into a non-diagnostic "products that may aggravate a Reactive Gut pattern" video, using the screen-door analogy and the locked line, "The foods aren't the problem. The barrier is." Keep language at "this pattern often shows up when," never "you have a leaky gut."
- **Sugar-Driven Gut (microbial overgrowth / candida pattern):** adapt Ben's blood-sugar and insulin content ("Blood Sugar Spikes Drop 52% If You Do THIS After Eating") into a video on why sugar cravings can feel bigger than willpower, using the squatters-in-the-house analogy and the locked line, "The cravings aren't yours. They're something else's, broadcasting through you." Frame as pattern, not diagnosis.
- **Underactive Gut (low stomach acid pattern):** adapt Ben's personal-experiment format ("I Drank 30 TBSP of Olive Oil in 10 Days," "I Took 5g of Creatine for 30 Days") into an Alina-led personal-trial video (for example, a 14-day digestion-support trial) using the stove-that-isn't-hot-enough analogy and the locked line, "The problem isn't what you're eating. It's that your stomach isn't breaking it down."
- **Bloated-and-Backed-Up Gut (SIBO pattern):** adapt Ben's "STOP doing X" imperative format together with his IBS-adjacent interview topic ("The #1 Cause of IBS, Bloating & Chronic Fatigue Nobody Talks About") into a "STOP doing these things if your bloating keeps coming back" video, using the wrong-neighborhood-plus-flooded-basement analogy and the locked line, "The bacteria aren't the problem. The location is." Emphasize the recurrence angle since that is what makes this pattern distinct, and stay in pattern language throughout, never "you have SIBO."
- **Numbered "brands to avoid" format:** this format performs consistently for Ben across four different product categories and translates cleanly to gut-relevant consumer picks Alina could credibly speak to (for example, gut-irritating additive categories, or supplement quality red flags), building trust through specificity without needing diagnostic claims.
- **News-jacking, used carefully:** Ben's RFK Jr and FDA-recall videos are among his single biggest hits. Alina can news-jack food-policy or food-safety stories relevant to gut health, but every pivot back to her content must stay in "this pattern often shows up when" language and avoid "cure" or "treat," since news-jacking content tends to reach cold, skeptical audiences who will scrutinize medical claims closely.
