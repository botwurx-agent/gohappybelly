## What this is, and what it isn't

Three days of bone broth instead of meals, with a group and daily check-ins.

**What it is:** a short, supported window where your digestive system is not doing its usual
job. Think of a kitchen that has been running service every day for years, with no closing
hours and no deep clean. Three days of broth is not starvation. It is the first time in a long
time that the kitchen gets to stop, while still being restocked with minerals, collagen, and
amino acids.

**What it isn't:** a detox, a cure, a weight loss plan, or a diagnosis. Your liver and kidneys
already handle detoxification and do not need help from me. This will not fix a gut pattern on
its own, and three days will not undo years of anything.

What it is genuinely good for is **information**. When you quiet a system down and then
reintroduce food slowly, whatever reacts is much easier to notice. Most people learn more about
their own pattern in the 48 hours *after* this reset than during it.

## The broth

### How much you need

**4 to 6 cups per day. 12 to 18 cups across the three days.**

That is 3 to 4.5 quarts total. Make or buy at least 4 quarts so you are not rationing on Day 3,
which is exactly when rationing makes people quit.

### Making it yourself

**For roughly 4 quarts:**

- 3 to 4 lbs bones (chicken carcasses and feet, or beef marrow and knuckle bones)
- 4 quarts cold filtered water, enough to cover the bones by about 2 inches
- 2 tablespoons apple cider vinegar
- 1 onion, quartered, skin on
- 2 carrots, roughly chopped
- 2 celery stalks, roughly chopped
- 1 head of garlic, halved crosswise
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 teaspoon black peppercorns
- Salt, added at the end, not the start

**Method:**

1. If using beef bones, roast them at 425F for 30 minutes first. This is optional for chicken
   but it deepens the flavour considerably, and flavour matters a lot when it is the only thing
   you are having for three days.
2. Put bones and vinegar in the pot with the cold water. Let it sit 30 minutes before heating.
   The vinegar helps draw minerals out of the bone.
3. Bring to a bare simmer. Do not boil. A rolling boil makes the broth cloudy and can turn the
   fats bitter.
4. Skim the grey foam off the top in the first hour.
5. Add vegetables, garlic, bay and peppercorns.
6. Simmer, lid slightly ajar: **chicken 8 to 12 hours, beef 12 to 24 hours.** A slow cooker on
   low or an Instant Pot on the soup setting both work and are easier to leave overnight.
7. Strain through a fine sieve. Cool, then refrigerate.
8. The fat will set on top. Leave it there while it stores, it seals the broth. Scrape most of
   it off before drinking.
9. Salt each cup as you drink it, to taste.

**You will know it is good broth if it gels in the fridge.** Wobbly like a soft jelly means
plenty of collagen came out. If it stays liquid it is still perfectly good to drink, it just
means fewer bones or less time. Not a problem, and not worth starting over.

**Storage:** 5 days in the fridge, 3 months in the freezer. Freeze in single-cup portions so
you are not defrosting a brick at 3pm on Day 2.

### Buying it instead

Completely fine. Plenty of people do a mix, and a buying week is better than a week where you
skip the reset because you could not face a stockpot.

**What to look for:**

- A short ingredient list: bones, water, vegetables, herbs, vinegar, salt
- It gels when cold
- Refrigerated or frozen, rather than shelf stable, where you have the choice
- Sodium you are comfortable with, since you will be having 4 to 6 cups a day

**What to skip:**

- Anything listing "natural flavors" or yeast extract high up the list
- Bouillon cubes, stock concentrates, and anything labelled "broth-flavoured"
- Anything with added sugar

Stock and broth are not the same thing. Stock is simmered briefly for cooking. You want broth,
simmered long, ideally collagen-rich.

## How much, and when

Spread the broth across the day rather than having it in two big hits. Roughly every 2 to 3
hours, so you are never more than a couple of hours from something warm.

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| On waking | Large glass of water with a pinch of salt and a squeeze of lemon |
| Breakfast | 1 cup broth, slowly |
| Mid-morning | Herbal tea, water |
| Lunch | 1 to 1.5 cups broth |
| Mid-afternoon | Electrolytes, water. This is the dip, plan for it |
| Dinner | 1.5 cups broth, sit down for it like a meal |
| Evening | Herbal tea, 1 cup broth if you want it |

### Sip, do not chug

A cup of broth drunk in thirty seconds sits heavily and satisfies nothing. The same cup over
fifteen minutes is warm, filling, and actually registers as a meal.

Sit down for it. Use a real mug. Do not drink it standing at the counter while answering email.
Three days is long enough that the ritual matters.

## Hydration and electrolytes

**This is most of the difference between a hard three days and a manageable one.**

Normally you get a surprising amount of your daily fluid, and nearly all of your sodium and
potassium, from food. Take food away and both drop, fast. Most of what people interpret as
hunger, headache, or weakness on Day 1 is actually mild dehydration and low sodium.

**Target:** 2.5 to 3 litres of total fluid a day, broth included. More if you are sweating or
it is hot.

**Electrolytes:** 1 to 2 servings a day, without added sugar. If you are active, in a hot
climate, or feeling weak, have the second one.

**A simple DIY version:** large glass of water, a good pinch of quality salt, a squeeze of
lemon. Twice a day. Broth itself is a decent sodium source once you salt it, which is part of
why we salt each cup rather than the pot.

**Do not** take potassium supplements on your own initiative. Get potassium from your
electrolyte product and your broth.

## What you can and cannot have

**Yes**

- Bone broth
- Water, still or sparkling
- Herbal tea: peppermint, ginger, chamomile, rooibos, nettle
- Electrolytes without sugar
- Salt, lemon, fresh herbs, spices in your broth

**One cup, if it is already your habit**

- Black coffee or plain green tea, in the morning only

Quitting caffeine at the same time as food is how you get a Day 1 headache that ruins the
whole day. Keep your usual morning cup, black. But know that caffeine on an empty stomach can
make jitters and anxiety worse, so if you feel wired and shaky, drop it.

**No**

- Any food, including "just a little something"
- Juice, smoothies, milk, plant milk, cream, sweeteners of any kind
- Alcohol
- Bouillon cubes and stock concentrate as a broth substitute
- Intense exercise. Walk, stretch, do gentle yoga. Skip heavy lifting, HIIT and long runs until
  Day 5.

## What is normal, and what is not

Print this page. Stick it on the fridge.

### Normal. Keep going.

| What you might feel | Why |
|---|---|
| Hunger arriving in waves, peaking then passing | Hunger is hormonal and cyclical, not a steadily rising line. A wave peaks around 20 minutes and passes if you drink something warm and wait |
| Mild headache, mostly Day 1 | Usually dehydration, low sodium, or caffeine change. Water and electrolytes first |
| Feeling cold | Less food means less thermic effect. Jumper, hot broth, blanket |
| Irritable, flat, or emotional | Very common Day 1 and 2. Food is regulation for most of us, and removing it shows you that |
| Tired, especially Day 1 afternoon | Expected. Reduce your commitments that day if you can |
| Poor sleep the first night or two | Common. It usually improves by night 3 |
| Fewer or no bowel movements | Nothing much is going in. Normal |
| Mild lightheadedness standing up quickly | Stand up slower, have electrolytes. Only a concern if it does not clear within a minute |
| Coated tongue, metallic taste, stronger breath | Very common and harmless |

### Stop and eat. Today.

If any of these show up, eat something small, cooked and simple, and message the group. This
round is not the right one for you and that is genuinely fine.

- Dizziness that does not clear within a minute of sitting down
- Fainting, or feeling like you are about to faint
- A racing, pounding, or irregular heartbeat
- Shaking you cannot settle
- Confusion, or trouble concentrating well beyond normal fog
- Changes to your vision
- A severe headache unlike your usual ones
- Vomiting, or being unable to keep fluids down
- Feeling genuinely, clearly unwell in a way that worries you

### Seek medical help now

Do not wait for the group, do not message me first.

- **Chest pain or pressure**
- **Fainting**
- **Confusion or disorientation**
- **Persistent vomiting, or an inability to keep any fluid down**
- **Breathing difficulty**

Nothing about three days of broth is worth risking your health over. Call your doctor or
emergency services.

## Breaking the fast

**This matters more than the three days you just did.**

Your digestion has been resting. Handing it a large, rich, complicated meal is the fastest way
to undo the work and feel worse than when you started. I have watched people do it and regret
it every single time.

### Your first meal, the morning after Day 3

Small. Cooked. Soft. Chewed properly.

Pick one:

- A soft-boiled or scrambled egg with a few spoons of well-cooked courgette
- A small bowl of white rice with broth poured over it
- Steamed carrot or squash, mashed, with a little olive oil
- Half an avocado with a pinch of salt

Portion size: about half what you would normally eat. Then **wait two to three hours** and
notice how you feel before the next thing.

### The next 48 hours

**Add back slowly:** cooked vegetables, eggs, white fish, chicken, rice, well-cooked grains,
small amounts of good fat.

**Leave until Day 6:** large raw salads and big volumes of roughage, dairy, alcohol, fried
food, very large meals, heavy sugar, and anything with a long ingredient list.

### The part most people miss

**This is the most useful window you have had in years.** Your system is quiet, so anything you
reintroduce speaks loudly. Keep a note on your phone for 48 hours: what you ate, what time,
and what you noticed in the two hours after.

A food that lands badly now is telling you something that would be invisible in your normal
week. That note is worth more than the three days.

## Troubleshooting

**I am starving and it is only 2pm on Day 1.**
Hot broth, slowly. A large glass of water with salt. Then move: a ten minute walk outside
shifts a hunger wave better than sitting and thinking about it. Post in the group. Day 1
afternoon is the single most common quit point and it is much easier with company.

**I am bored of the taste.**
Fresh ginger, a squeeze of lemon, cracked pepper, a teaspoon of miso, a pinch of turmeric, a
dash of hot sauce without sugar. Alternate chicken and beef broth if you have both.

**I have a headache that will not shift.**
Water and electrolytes first, twice, an hour apart. If you dropped caffeine, have your usual
morning coffee tomorrow. If it is severe or unlike your usual headaches, stop and eat.

**I cannot sleep.**
Common on nights 1 and 2. Have a cup of broth an hour before bed rather than going to sleep
with nothing. Keep the room cool and skip screens late. It usually settles by night 3.

**I broke the fast by accident.**
Nothing is ruined. Have a proper small meal, then pick up where you left off at the next
broth. This is three days, not a purity test.

**I had to stop completely.**
Then you listened to your body, which is the whole skill. Come to the closing call anyway.
What made you stop is information about your pattern, and I would rather you understood what
it was telling you.
