## What this tells us

1. **Character count is the real guardrail, not word count.** Ben's single biggest hits cluster around **54 to 62 characters**. That is not an accident: YouTube truncates a title with "..." at roughly 60 characters on mobile and in the suggested-video sidebar, which is where most views come from. His winners fit before the cut.
2. **His biggest wins are SHORTER than his average upload.** Ben's recent uploads run ~67 chars, but his all-time top third runs ~54 chars, and the length-to-views correlation on his proven winners is a clear negative (-0.40). Shorter, punchier titles are what broke out.
3. **The sweet spot is roughly 6 to 11 words and under about 60 characters.** Most of Ben's solo titles land 8 to 13 words; his hits skew to the short end of that.
4. **Ben front-loads and is declarative.** "Blood Sugar Spikes Drop 52%," "STOP Drinking These 5 Coffees," "First 60 Minutes After Waking Decides Whether You Burn Or Store Fat." Specific numbers, a hard claim, the hook in the first few words. No hedging.
5. **Carmichael confirms the structures, not the length target for us.** His niche is business, his format is long compilations, so his content does not transfer. But his title structures match Ben's: numbered lists, specific-number claims, curiosity gaps, authority names. It reinforces the method, not the exact length.

## Our titles: the good news and the fix

**Good news:** our median (10 words / 57 chars) is already right in Ben's range. The problem is not the whole set. It is two specific things:

**Problem 1, a handful run long and would truncate.** Anything over ~62 characters gets cut off in the feed.
**Problem 2, the long ones hedge.** "Might Help," "a Simple... to Try," "That Might Be Working" add length and drain the punch. We hedged for compliance caution, but we can be punchy and still non-diagnostic (Ben is not making medical claims either; he is making curiosity claims).

## The rule going forward (added to the Reference Playbook)

- **Target 6 to 11 words and 60 characters or fewer.** Hard ceiling ~65. Check the character count, not just the word count.
- **Front-load the hook in the first 5 words** so it survives truncation in the mobile feed and the suggested sidebar.
- **Cut hedge words** (might, could, may, simple, a few, some, help). Be declarative and specific while staying non-diagnostic: a curiosity or benefit claim, not a medical claim.
- **Prefer a specific number or a hard, concrete noun** over a soft description.
