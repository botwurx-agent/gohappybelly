## [BODY]

### Point 1: The engine timing problem (3:15 to 6:00)

[SPOKEN]: Let me explain what I mean by timing, because this is the whole video in one idea.

[SPOKEN]: Picture a car engine. When the timing is right, the spark fires at exactly the right moment in the cycle, and everything runs smooth. When the timing is off, even a little, the same fuel, the same spark plug, the same engine produces knocking, stalling, rough idle. Nothing about the fuel changed. The sequence did.

[SPOKEN]: Your nervous system runs your digestion the same way. Stomach acid, digestive enzymes, gut motility, they are all supposed to fire in a sequence, and that sequence is controlled by whether your nervous system thinks it is safe to rest and digest, or whether it thinks it needs to be on alert. When you are chronically stressed, under-slept, and running on adrenaline just to get through a normal Tuesday, that system starts firing out of sequence before you have had a single sip of coffee.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: THE ENGINE TIMING IS OFF

[SPOKEN]: This is what I call the Wired-and-Tired pattern, and here is the line I want you to remember, because it explains everything that is about to make sense.

[SPOKEN]: The food is fine. The system processing it isn't.

[RETENTION LOOP]: This is the locked aha line, deliver it slowly. It reframes coffee, food, and everything that follows.

### Point 2: Habit 1, drinking it fasted, first thing (6:00 to 9:00)

[SPOKEN]: Habit one. You wake up and the very first thing that goes into your body is coffee, on a completely empty stomach, before you have eaten anything at all.

[SPOKEN]: Here is why that lands so hard on a Wired-and-Tired system specifically. Caffeine tells your adrenal glands to release cortisol and adrenaline. That is literally the mechanism, that is how it wakes you up. If your nervous system is already leaning stressed, you are adding a stress hormone spike on top of a stress hormone spike, on a stomach that has nothing in it to buffer any of it. Stomach acid ramps up with nowhere to go. Your gut gets the signal to speed up before it has anything to actually move. That combination is exactly what shows up as that fast, uncomfortable, almost immediate bloating and stomach upset some women get within twenty minutes of their first sip.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: FASTED COFFEE = DOUBLE STRESS SIGNAL

[SPOKEN]: Notice this is not "coffee is acidic, therefore bad." Plenty of women drink coffee on an empty stomach their whole lives with zero issue, because their system was not already running wired and tired to begin with. Same fuel, different engine. That is the whole point.

[VISUAL NOTE]: Split screen, a mug next to an empty plate versus a mug next to a small bowl of food. Keep tone matter-of-fact, not alarming.

### Point 3: Habit 2, drinking it too early and too much (9:00 to 11:45)

[SPOKEN]: Habit two, and this one surprises people. It is not just that you drink coffee early, it is how early, and how much, relative to what your body is already doing on its own.

[SPOKEN]: Your body actually produces its own natural cortisol spike in the first thirty to sixty minutes after you wake up, it is called the cortisol awakening response, and it is supposed to be what gets you moving. If you are chugging a big coffee the second your eyes open, you are stacking a caffeine-driven cortisol spike directly on top of your body's own natural one, at the exact moment it is already highest. For a Wired-and-Tired system, that is not a gentle nudge awake, that is two alarms going off at once.

[SPOKEN]: And then there is volume. One cup on a calm system is very different from three cups by ten a.m. on a system that is already running hot. Each cup is another spark firing into an engine that has not had a chance to settle from the last one. The bloating, the jitteriness, the wired-but-exhausted feeling by lunchtime, a lot of that is simply too much fuel, too fast, on top of a system that was already misfiring.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: TWO ALARMS AT ONCE

[SPOKEN]: This is not about never having a second cup. It is about noticing whether your system can actually handle the volume and timing you are giving it, or whether it is already maxed out before coffee even enters the picture.

[VISUAL NOTE]: A simple graphic, two rising lines (natural cortisol curve, caffeine curve) stacking into one tall spike. Keep it clean, not clinical.

### Point 4: Habit 3, stacking it on poor sleep (11:45 to 14:15)

[SPOKEN]: Habit three, and this is the one I think matters most, because it is the one almost nobody connects to their gut. Drinking coffee, especially in the way we just described, on top of a night of bad or short sleep.

[SPOKEN]: When you are under-slept, your nervous system is already starting the day in that alert, on-guard state, before coffee touches it. Poor sleep alone raises your baseline cortisol and throws off the natural rhythm your gut relies on to know when to rest and digest versus when to stay on alert. So you are not pouring caffeine into a calm, well-rested system that can absorb the jolt. You are pouring it into a system that spent the whole night already firing out of sequence, and now you are asking it to also process food on top of that.

[SPOKEN]: This is why the exact same cup of coffee can feel totally fine on a week where you slept well, and absolutely wreck your stomach on a week where you did not. It was never really about the coffee being different. It was about how much timing damage was already there before the coffee showed up.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: BAD SLEEP LOADS THE ENGINE FIRST

[SPOKEN]: And this is exactly why "just cut out coffee" so often does not fix it. If sleep is the real driver, you can quit coffee entirely and still bloat, because the underlying timing problem never got addressed.

[VISUAL NOTE]: A tired woman scrolling her phone late at night, then the same woman reaching for coffee the next morning. Warm, relatable, not shaming.

### Point 5: How to keep your coffee and fix the timing (14:15 to 16:45)

[SPOKEN]: So here is the one small change I promised you, and then three more you can layer on if you want to go further.

[SPOKEN]: The single biggest lever is this: do not let coffee be the first thing that enters your body. Have a few sips of water first, and if you can, a small bite of something with protein or fat in it before your first cup, even just a few bites. That alone gives your system a buffer so caffeine is not landing on a completely empty, already-alert stomach. For a lot of women this one change quiets down most of the fast, within-twenty-minutes bloating almost immediately.

[SPOKEN]: Second, give yourself sixty to ninety minutes after waking before that first cup, so you are working with your natural cortisol rise instead of piling directly on top of it. Third, notice your actual volume. You do not need to quit coffee, you need to notice if you are somewhere around cup three by mid-morning on a system that is already wired, and consider whether cup one or two would actually get you the same lift with a lot less backfire. And fourth, on the nights you know sleep was rough, treat the next morning as a lower-caffeine morning on purpose, because that is the day your system has the least room to absorb the jolt.

[TEXT OVERLAY]: EAT FIRST, WAIT, WATCH THE VOLUME

[SPOKEN]: None of this is about fear or restriction. It is about giving your engine's timing a chance to catch up before you ask it to also process a stimulant. Most women who make just the first change, eating or drinking water before that first cup, notice a real difference within a few days.
