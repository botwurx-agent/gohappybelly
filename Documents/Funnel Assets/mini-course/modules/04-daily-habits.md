# Module 04: Everyday Habits That Support Digestion

## Why Small And Boring Wins

Nothing in this module is clever. That is the point.

Your gut runs on rhythm. It has a daily cycle, it anticipates meals, it repairs
on a schedule. What disrupts it is not usually one bad thing, it is
irregularity: eating at random times, sleeping badly three nights a week,
starting a new regimen every two weeks.

So the goal is not intensity. It is predictability. One habit held for a month
does more than five habits held for nine days.

## Food

Real, colorful, fiber-rich, most of the time. Fewer processed foods and less
added sugar. Enough fat and enough protein.

That is genuinely most of it. The sophistication comes later and it comes from
knowing your pattern, not from a longer list of rules.

## Fiber

Fiber is what feeds the bacteria that keep your colon lining fed. Aim for 25 to
35 grams a day.

**Go up slowly.** This matters more than the target. Jumping from 12 grams to 35
in a week produces gas, bloating and the conclusion that fiber is bad for you.
Add a few grams at a time, and drink more water as you do, because fiber without
water is worse than neither.

## Water

Water is involved in the enzyme activity and the muscular movement that push
things along. Dehydration shows up as sluggish digestion before it shows up as
thirst.

If you drink a lot of water and still feel dry, electrolytes are worth a look
before more water is.

## Sleep

Seven to nine hours, and more importantly, roughly the same hours.

Your gut does its repair work overnight, and your bacteria run on a daily clock
of their own. Shift that clock around and you shift them with it. The person who
sleeps six hours consistently often does better than the one who alternates
between four and ten.

## Stress

Stress reduces stomach acid and slows movement. Not metaphorically. Your body
down-prioritizes digestion when it believes something more urgent is happening,
and it cannot tell the difference between a predator and your inbox.

Three slow breaths before you eat changes which nervous system state you are in
when the food arrives. It takes twenty seconds and it is the highest return
thing in this module.

## Movement

Walking moves things along. That is the mechanism, and it is enough. Ten minutes
after a meal beats an hour at the gym at 7am for this particular purpose.

> **Alina's take.** Everyone wants the protocol. Almost nobody is doing these
> six things consistently, and I have watched people spend eight hundred dollars
> on supplements to work around a habit they could have fixed for free. I am not
> saying supplements never matter. I am saying they work much better on top of
> this than instead of it.

## The Daily Checklist

- Real food with fiber
- Water, and electrolytes if you need them
- Consistent sleep hours
- Three breaths before meals
- A short walk after eating
