this batch; "Who Is Teaching This" carries Alina's photograph and has no hero.

**Model: `nano_banana_2_1`, 16:9, 2k, `thinking_level: high`**, per
`Website/mockups/patterns/README.md`.

## Why this batch is all photography and no illustration

Every one of the twelve is an analogy whose object exists in the world: a gate,
a smoke alarm, a sink, a tree. The allocation rule in
`Website/IMAGE-DIRECTION.md` sends those to **photography of the real object**,
and sends only the inside of the body to illustration. Nothing here is inside
the body, so nothing here is drawn.

## The shared style suffix

Appended to all twelve. The palette lives in the **walls, objects and light,
never in clothing**, which is the trap from the earlier GPT set.

> Photorealistic photograph, 50mm lens, natural daylight, soft and directional.
> Quiet domestic realism, lived-in rather than styled. Muted palette of sage
> green, warm cream, soft terracotta and pale oak, carried in the surfaces and
> the light. Fine film grain, believable shadow falloff, slight wear and
> asymmetry. Absolutely no text, lettering, numbers, labels, logos, barcodes or
> printed packaging anywhere in the frame. Not an illustration, not a render.

## The direction traps, and why two of the three do not apply here

The patterns batch needed "looking away from the lens" and "no hand pressed to
her stomach". **There is no person in any of these twelve**, so both traps are
out of reach by construction.

The third one is live and is the whole risk of this batch: **Nano Banana is a
weak instruction follower on text.** A smoke alarm has TEST printed on it, a
music stand holds notation, a shoe carries a maker's mark, a jar has a label.
Each prompt names the no-text condition on the specific object rather than
relying on the suffix. Check every returned frame at full size.

**No text appeared anywhere in the set.** That is better than the patterns
batch, where the no-text rule had to be fought object by object. The likely
reason is that naming the condition on the specific object ("no test button
label", "no paper label, no printed lid") works where a blanket suffix does not.

### The one weak frame

`h09-instruments` is the weakest and is being kept anyway. The two bows on the
floor are bare sticks with no hair, and the cello at the left edge is cropped
closely enough to read as a fragment. Both are only visible at full size, and
re-rolling instrument anatomy on this model reliably trades one fault for a
worse one. If it is ever re-shot, drop the bows from the prompt rather than
describing them better.

### Tonal range is wider than the patterns set

Nine of the twelve sit in the cream-and-sage daylight the palette describes.
Three do not, by design: `h07-two-houses` is dusk, `h12-lights-on` is night,
and `h09-instruments` is amber stage light. They will read as a deliberate
change of register on the two bonus lessons and the closing one, not as a
mismatch, because each is the last image in its run.

Steve: the instruments did not translate. He kept the metaphor and rejected the
frame, correctly: "the picture just looked like instruments sitting next to one
another." His direction was an orchestra setting, one instrument per chair,

**The orchestra is also in the body copy**, as Alina's take:

> Think of it as an orchestra. Every habit is one instrument, and none of them

`search_course_content` across both courses returns that one line and nothing
else, so the metaphor is contained to this lesson. Because Steve kept the

would have had to be rewritten in the same pass, or the picture would have
argued with the line the way the first Sugar-Driven frame did.

### Chosen: C3, 2026-10-27

Steve picked C3. The staging problem he spotted was real and the stands were
reversed, not merely misplaced: a stand belongs in front of the player, between
them and the conductor, so from the audience side the backs of the stands
should be visible with the chairs behind them.

C1 corrected that and showed why correcting it is the wrong move here. Four
dark stands then sit across the middle of the frame and clip the trumpet and
the cello. The staging is right and the subject is lost.

C3 keeps the stands Steve wanted for the scene, in the rows behind rather than
the front row, where the mass of chairs and stands receding into the dark is
what actually produces the orchestra. The front four read clean. An empty front
desk with its stand not yet set is unremarkable.

Front row checked at native resolution: violin with bow, flute with correct
keywork, trumpet with three valves, cello. Four different instruments as asked,
no text, nobody present.
